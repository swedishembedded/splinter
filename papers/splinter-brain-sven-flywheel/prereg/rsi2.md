# Registration of RSI2: recursive self-improvement by on-policy learning, judged per lineage on a sealed exam

Written on 2026-11-13, before any run of RSI2. The design, exam, criterion and analysis below are fixed by this
commit. The exact builds (splinter, sven, brain commits and binary digests) and the training settings resolved from
the defaults named here are recorded in a launch record committed before generation 1 of any lineage; anything
changed after that is reported as a dated deviation beside the outcome, which is reported whatever it is.

## Why a second registration

RSI1 showed self-generated, interpreter-relabelled supervised training on Qwen3-4B lifting an exam the base never
answered, but third-generation steps did not hold: arm HL met its per-lineage criterion in 1 of 4 lineages (r2 fell
at generation 3, 27 wins / 36 losses; r1 stalled; outcome entry of 2026-11-13 in `recursive-improvement.md`). The
diagnosis recorded since (`.agents/roadmap/recursive-improvement.md`, findings 143-145, and the gap analysis of
2026-11-13) points at the training signal, not at the loop: six optimiser steps per round at lr 1e-6 on 3-4 records,
acceptance on 3-4 underpowered probes, no frontier control, and a retention bound that either let losses through or
rolled real gains back. RSI2 keeps the exam and the per-lineage test and replaces the update with on-policy learning
under the campaign controller, with powered gates.

## Design

- **Policy:** Qwen/Qwen3-4B, the same base for all lineages. Thinking off (as the exam protocol).
- **Lineages:** four, differing only in the controller's seed. Each lineage runs three generations from the base.
- **What a generation is:** one campaign arm of the CampaignController (splinter `pipelines::campaign`) started from the
  previous generation's champion (generation 1 from the base), with a fixed budget of optimiser steps registered in
  the launch record. Every training episode of a generation is produced by that generation's current policy (on-policy);
  no stronger model, teacher or human data enters training. The generation's champion is its last policy accepted by
  the controller's dev-validation gate; a generation with no accepted policy keeps its predecessor as champion.
- **Tasks the policy learns on:** Lua 5.1 programs whose printed output is to be predicted, (a) proposed by the current
  policy itself from the Lua 5.1 reference manual (splinter's self-generation of the `predict-lua` kind, as in RSI1 arm
  H) and (b) generated by the environment factory's program-output plugin. The Lua 5.1 interpreter is the only oracle.
  Dynamic sampling keeps only tasks the current policy solves sometimes (k = 8 attempts per task); tasks the policy
  always solves (pass rate >= 0.9) are retired.
- **Update:** policy gradient on the policy's own graded attempts with group-standardised advantages, asymmetric
  clip (clip-higher), behaviour log-probabilities as recorded at sampling, brain's per-step KL and gradient-norm guard.
  Exact values (learning rate, steps, group size, batch, clip edges, guard bounds, KV precision of the sampling server)
  are fixed in the launch record and are the same for every lineage and generation.
- **Agent runtime:** training episodes are executed by sven (`sven rollout` over task folders, policy traces with
  exact token ids and log-probabilities) against brain's serving endpoint, where the controller's served path is
  available at launch; otherwise in process. The launch record states which, and it is the same for all lineages.
- **Gates inside the loop:** a dev-validation set and a retention set, both sized by the controller's power-first
  planning for the effect each must detect (retention as non-inferiority with a stated margin). Neither contains exam
  items. The loop never sees the exam.

## Exam (sealed)

Arm H's exam: 889 code-written Lua 5.1 items in 25 standard-library families that the base never answered under arm
H's protocol (bench `environments/predict`, exam `lua51-rsi1`, references re-derived by the pinned interpreter). It is
held by bench and is never available to the learning loop. Every champion and the base are measured on all 889 items
under one protocol, on one serving build, in one measurement session per comparison: one greedy completion, neutral
request, thinking off, the answer read from the last fenced block, compared token-wise with whitespace normalised (arm
H's protocol). The base is re-measured on the RSI2 build; it is not taken from RSI1.

## Criterion

For each lineage and each step (generation 1 vs base, generation 2 vs generation 1, generation 3 vs generation 2), a
win is an item the later model answers and the earlier does not, a loss the reverse. A step holds when wins exceed
losses and the one-sided exact sign test gives p < 0.05. A lineage succeeds when all three of its steps hold (a
generation that kept its predecessor's champion makes its step fail). **RSI2 meets its criterion when at least three
of the four lineages succeed.** Pooled sign tests per step are reported beside it, and so are the dev and retention
trajectories, informative-group fractions, KL and clip statistics and GPU-hours.

## Secondary outcomes (reported, not part of the criterion)

- The same comparisons through sven: bench `predict` collection of the same 889 items run by sven against each
  champion, judged by `bench verdict` (paired, family-clustered).
- Forgetting: losses per step on the exam, and the retention set.
- Tokens per solved item.

## What is not claimed

Nothing is claimed until the criterion is evaluated. A lineage that falls or stalls is reported with its numbers;
the outcome is recorded whatever it is.

## Clarification before any run (2026-11-13, no generation has run)

Who does what, so that training never sees the examination:

- **Bench is the only examiner.** It holds the exam, serves each candidate itself on its own brain build, runs the
  registered protocol, keeps every item-level grade, computes each step's sign test and the per-lineage criterion,
  and returns signed aggregate verdicts only (items right, wins and losses, p, step holds, criterion met). It also
  audits the training tasks each generation used for overlap with the exam and returns only a count.
- **The learning side (splinter, sven, brain as the learner's engine) never reads the exam** or any file derived from
  it, for any purpose: no deduplication against it, no measurement of it, no access to its grades. Dev and retention
  sets come only from the learner's own generators and proposals.
- The learner hands bench, per lineage and generation, a candidate (base and adapter digests, serving settings,
  commits) and the list of training tasks; nothing flows from bench to the learner except the aggregate verdicts. The
  base is generation 0 and is measured by bench on the same build as every champion.
