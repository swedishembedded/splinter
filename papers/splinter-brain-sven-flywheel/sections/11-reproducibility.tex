\section{Reproducibility}\label{sec:repro}

\paragraph{What is needed.} Brain, Sven and Splinter, cloned from their remotes, and the evidence
dataset \code{swedishembedded/the-frontier-must-grow-evidence}. The dataset holds only what this
paper cites: the 889-item exam, the examiner's signed step and plan verdicts and the rule it applied,
the learner's round records, every candidate record with its hand-off list of programs, the twelve
S4 champion adapters, S1's analyses and per-model grades, and the records of the documentation runs
and the 27B generations, one archive per series. \code{reproduce/dataset.json} pins the dataset's
revision and the SHA-256 of every file and archive member, and \code{data/measurements.json} names
each cited record by its path in the unpacked dataset and its digest. Every commit named below is
contained in a branch of its repository's remote; \code{make verify} fails on a pin that is not.

\paragraph{Three tiers.} The targets are run in the paper's directory
(\code{make -C} with the paper's directory); downloads, builds and outputs land
under \code{target/papers}, and \code{EVIDENCE\_DIR}, \code{REPRO}, \code{BRAIN\_SRC}, \code{SVEN\_SRC},
\code{DEVICE} and \code{LOCK} move them, name the clones and the card (\code{make reproduce} lists
the tiers).

\paragraph{The reference examiner.} \code{reproduce/examiner.py} implements the examination protocol
of \cref{sec:rsi-design} from that specification: the neutral request (the item's instruction and the
sentence ``Answer with exactly what it prints, in one fenced code block.''), no system prompt,
thinking off, one greedy completion of at most 2048 tokens, the last complete fenced block or else the
whole reply as the answer, a reply cut at the limit graded as written, the comparison token by token
per line with whitespace runs collapsed and blank lines ignored, the exclusion of every item equal to
a program on either compared model's hand-off list (exactly, or once comments and whitespace are
removed), the exact one-sided sign test, the paired gain with its template-clustered Student-$t$
interval ($G-1$ degrees of freedom) and the registered per-lineage plan rule. It checks the served
base against the declared base digest and each adapter file against its declared digest, and
serves one lineage at a time, because adapters of different lineages carry the same card ids. Its
unit tests run in \code{make verify}. It serves on the learner's Brain \code{796871647}: the build the
published verdicts were served on (binary SHA-256 \code{d11e9134...}) was made from a Brain source
that is not in Brain's published history.

\paragraph{What a reader cannot reproduce.} (1) The fresh exam F of the registered follow-ups and its
generator are not released; when F's verdicts exist, only their signed aggregates are published.
(2) The published verdicts were produced by a different implementation of the examiner than the
reference one, on a different Brain build, so \code{reproduce/examine} re-measures the result rather
than replaying the published grades; a difference within serving nondeterminism is expected, and
the comparison reports it. (3) The verdicts are signed with a symmetric HMAC key, so a reader cannot
verify a signature; the signature guards against the learner, not against the authors.
(4) \code{reproduce/rerun} is a statistical replication, not a bit-identical one: proposals and
frontier predictions are sampled at temperature 1.0 and GPU kernels are not deterministic across
hosts, so the reproduced lineages are new draws of the same procedure. (5) The raw logs of the
training-substrate qualification and throughput probes, a trajectory probe, and the coding-task pilots
and probes were not kept; \code{data/measurements.json} records their values, digests and commands, marked
\code{"kept": false}, and \code{make verify} reports them as not kept.

\paragraph{Verification.} \code{make verify} first runs the specifications of the pin rule, of the
record parsers and of the reference examiner, then re-runs every structure check against the commits
in \code{data/commits.json}, then \code{scripts/build\_data.py --check}, which resolves each measured
and computed claim to its value, requires the claim text to contain the value, requires every ledger
row to be cited in the text and every citation to be in the ledger, and checks that the pooled steps
computed from the examiner's lineage verdicts equal its plan verdicts; with \code{EVIDENCE=DIR} it
also re-extracts every value from the records in \code{DIR}. The ledger holds \mLedgerClaims{}
claims, \mLedgerStructure{} of them structure claims backed by \mLedgerChecks{} checks. The sibling
repositories are given by \code{--sven} and \code{--brain}. \code{make check} includes this
verification as \code{check/papers}. The bibliography entries were checked against the archive's
abstract pages on 2026-11-15.

\paragraph{Draft and final.} The paper's status is recorded in \code{status.txt}; when it reads
final, \code{build\_data.py --check} also fails while any registered-but-unmeasured or planned claim
remains, so a final version holds only structure facts, measurements, computations from measurements
and citations. This version is a draft because one planned claim remains: the two registered S4
follow-ups, whose results it will report when they exist.\cl{RS39}

\paragraph{Registrations.} All registrations are in the paper's \code{prereg/} directory: the
coding-task protocol in \code{coding-tasks.md}, the acquisition experiments in \code{acquisition.md},
S1 in \code{recursive-improvement.md}, and S2 to S4 each in its own file (\code{rsi4.md} for
S4) with a launch record (\code{rsi4-launch.md}) that fixes builds, binary digests, settings and
seeds before the first generation and carries the series' dated deviations; the outcome of each series
is appended to its registration.

\paragraph{Build.} From the repository root the paper is built by
\begin{center}\code{make papers/splinter-brain-sven-flywheel/pdf}\end{center}
into \code{target/papers}; every table, plot and number of the series S1--S4 and of the coding-task pilots
is generated from \code{data/} into the build directory.
The same build writes \code{target/papers/splinter-brain-sven-flywheel-arxiv.tar.gz}, the source archive
for arXiv: the main file, the sections, the generated tables, macros and plot data at the paths the
source names, the bibliography and its \code{.bbl}, with comments removed. The build compiles the
archive in an empty directory with \code{pdflatex} and \code{bibtex} and with \code{latexmk}, and
fails unless both give the main PDF's page count with no error or undefined reference; the archive is
uploaded as it is.
