\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{What the structure claims show.} They show that definitions, constants, tests and messages
exist at the pinned commits; they do not show that the code behaves accordingly. Behaviour is
established only by the measurements of
\cref{sec:substrate,sec:coding-results,sec:documentation,sec:generations,sec:rsi}. The qualification
of the 27B training substrate used random weights for the long sequences and a handful of examples for
the real checkpoint, and no sequence longer than 16384 tokens was run. The requirements of
\cref{sec:requirements} include one that is not implemented: the retention check does not test what
each release demonstrably acquired, so for a domain of facts a passed retention check is not evidence
that taught facts were kept. The workspace verifier's reading of the runner's report and the practice
gate's canaries exist at the current head, not at the revision the architecture claims are pinned
to.\cl{OP04,OP05} The corpus expansion mechanisms (variants, retention queue, rehearsal, claims) are
described from the code and none was evaluated for the value it adds.

\paragraph{Single greedy runs and run-to-run variation.} The development probes of \cref{sec:coding-results} (the supervision ablation, the recovery dose-response and the generation-2 rehearsal ablation) are one run per arm at greedy decoding. The same adapter evaluated three times on the same benchmark decoded 30, 31 and 26 of 46 tasks, and the three reports' verdicts differ pairwise on 9, 12 and 11 of the 46 tasks; a single greedy arm therefore carries about 2.5 tasks of standard deviation, so the paired p-values of single-run comparisons overstate their precision.\cl{REP1} The variation is two different things, and the decode batch is only one of them.\cl{REP2} A single-row decode is reproducible: five sequential identical task-derived requests, sent greedily with the requested seed echoed by the server, returned identical completion token ids and bitwise identical per-token log-probabilities, and a never-seen prompt sent once cold and then twice warm was identical both times. A row's arithmetic does depend on its co-batch: six or eight identical requests sent at once return two distinct sequences, because the int8 projections run a block that is bit-identical below five rows and switch to an int8 tensor-core block from five, while the fused decode attention sizes its split-key count from the batch. But one request in flight does not make a whole episode reproducible either: the same adapter on the same eight tasks, greedy at concurrency one, passed three and then five of eight with two verdicts flipped. The registered multi-seed protocol is therefore the instrument for agent episodes. Training is not bit-reproducible either: two runs of one recipe with the same binary, corpus, start and seed produced two distinct adapters whose losses agree to the fourth digit while their gradient norms and weights do not.\cl{REP3} A same-seed comparison therefore means the same data, recipe and binary, not a bit-identical run. The RSI examiner's protocol is greedy at one request per item on one build and tier, and its base-against-base null replicate found no discordant item (\cref{sec:rsi-design}).

\paragraph{The coding-task experiment.} The registered confirmatory query does not show the claim
(\cref{sec:coding-results}), and the same-seed supervision contrast that does separate is a secondary
analysis, not the registered decision. Registered experiments that were not run: the ten-stage
training and deployment experiment for the 27B model (CE6), the replication of the unchanged procedure
on a second independent repository (CE7), and the acquisition, choice-of-improvement and generations
experiments of \code{prereg/acquisition.md} (ACQ1, INT1, GEN1). The paper therefore reports no
replication of the headline, no result of the registered retention check or of the three controls of
\cref{sec:protocol}, and no result of ACQ1 (the documentation runs of \cref{sec:documentation} were not
run under that registration). The memorisation guard of CE8 as registered reads one step's training
loss and any rise at the last evaluation, so it can stop a run on noise; it was kept unchanged.\cl{M48,M49}
Both pilots of the adapter are inside the same variation the base shows against itself on a second
protocol seed.\cl{M57} The registration fixes one model size for the first training result (Qwen3-8B),
trained on a stronger teacher's verified trajectories because the untrained 8B produced too few edits
to supply its own, so a gain there would be distillation from the teacher, not
self-improvement;\cl{PR15,PR16} one agent harness; and process isolation \code{none} on the node (a
solver's commands are confined by the kernel to their tree and have no network, but the node is not a
container; \cref{sec:audit}).\cl{AU09,CN01,CN08} Mutation-seeded tasks test whether a model can undo a
synthetic fault, not whether it can debug a historical one. A family is the file a fault was seeded in
for the authored repositories, so clusters are files, not subsystems.\cl{PR02} The base model may have
seen public repositories in pretraining; the paired base arm bounds that effect, it does not remove it.

\paragraph{The 27B generations.} Two generations were run, not the three planned, on three of four
workloads, on exams the base already solved about nine times in ten, so the few tasks it solved only
sometimes carried every comparison.\cl{G2703} The token-efficiency finding was made after the data
over several comparisons and is exploratory.\cl{G2705}

\paragraph{The self-improvement series.} \Cref{sec:rsi-scope} states what the series does and does not
establish; the limits that affect how far the result can be trusted are these. The registered test
treats the exam's items as independent; under the examiner's template-clustered intervals the third
generation's gain excludes zero in one lineage, or two under a one-sided reading, not in the three the
rule asks for (\cref{sec:rsi-robust}).\cl{RS38} One exam served all four series, and each design was
chosen after the results of the one before on it, so the series as a whole reused a holdout
adaptively.\cl{CT22} The exam measures generalisation within its 25 templates, to items the base did not
answer at selection; the overlap exclusion catches only exact and comment- or whitespace-normalised
matches, and a similarity audit that abstracts arguments or syntax was not run. Training data grows
with the generation, and the matched-data control that would separate that from an improved proposer
has not run, nor has the re-examination on a freshly drawn exam; both are registered.\cl{RS39} Items-right
levels and the coding-task and 27B means are reported as point estimates with their paired tests;
intervals are given where the records allow them (\cref{fig:rsi-forest}, \cref{sec:documentation}).
The examiner is a separate program run by the same operators, its verdicts are signed with a symmetric
key that protects them against the learner and not against the authors, and RSI1's arms were graded by
their own analysis scripts. The learner's dev gate, reused across generations, accepted two RSI3 steps
the exam did not hold, and the gate that would control such acceptances was not used in any
run.\cl{RS24,RS34}
