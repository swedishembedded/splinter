\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{What the result is about.} One task family (predicting what Lua~5.1 programs print), one base
model with thinking off, three rounds of LoRA training on interpreter labels, and one exam of 25
templates selected on the base's failures and reused while the four series were designed; nothing is
claimed about other tasks, models, longer series or thinking-on use, and no baseline yet sizes the
effect against the base with reasoning or a larger sibling.

\paragraph{What the statistics support.} The registered test is item-level and supports claims about
pairs of adapters; at the template level the third round holds in one or two of four lineages; at the
procedure level four lineages are a replication count.\cl{RS38,RS53} The step-1 test is inflated by
the exam's selection, churn between adapters has no measured noise floor, and the training data grows
with the round, so more verified data and a better proposer are not yet separated; the registered
follow-ups address each of these.\cl{RS39} The overlap exclusion catches exact and comment- or
whitespace-normalised matches only. The examiner is a separate program run by the same operators, its
verdicts are signed with a symmetric key that protects them against the learner and not against the
authors, and it reports no per-template counts and no truncation counts; it tolerated no failed request
and none occurred.\cl{RS50} The learner's dev gate advanced on any net gain and filtered nothing.\cl{RS49}

\paragraph{The rest of the paper.} The limits of the system specification and of the coding, 27B and
documentation experiments are in \cref{app:limits}.
