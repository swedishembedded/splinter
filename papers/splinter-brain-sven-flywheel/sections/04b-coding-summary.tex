\section{Repository tasks with a stronger teacher: the confirmatory query}\label{sec:coding-summary}

\paragraph{Question and design.} Can verified engineering-agent experience improve completion of
unseen repository tasks?\cl{PR09} The registered test (\cref{sec:protocol}, registered before any run)
trains an 8B student on the verified trajectories a 27B teacher produced on development families and
compares it with the untrained 8B on a frozen qualification set of 46 task families, each task solved
at three registered decoding seeds, by a one-sided exact sign test over families at
$\alpha=0.05$.\cl{PR15,PR04}

\paragraph{Outcome: not shown.} The candidate registered before the query, the decisive-action adapter
of training seed 13, solved 23.0 of the 46 tasks on average over the three seeds against 20.0 for its
base, winning 18 families, losing 12 and tying 16 ($p=0.18$): the candidate is not demonstrated better
than its base.\cl{QUAL1} It had been chosen on a six-seed development read in which it solved 24.5
against the base's 19.67 ($p=0.040$), so part of that margin was selection.\cl{PROTO6} A secondary
contrast did separate: against its all-action control, trained on the same corpus with the same recipe,
seed and binary but supervising every action instead of only file edits and writes, it won 26 families
and lost 11 ($p=0.010$). The control itself solved 16.33, below the base, so the contrast shows that
supervising every action does harm rather than that supervising decisive actions helps.\cl{QUAL1}

\paragraph{What else ran.} Before the query, pilots of the headline and of a 27B model trained on its
own trajectories showed no held-out improvement on a 23-task subset, and the base solved twice at two
protocol seeds differed from itself by as much as either adapter did from it.\cl{M47,M52,M53,M56,M57}
\Cref{app:coding} holds the protocol, the pilots, the development probes and the run-to-run variation
they exposed.
