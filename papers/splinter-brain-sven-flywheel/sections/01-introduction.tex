\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Can a small local model get better, round after round, by training on material it proposes itself,
when every label comes from an executable oracle and no stronger model or human answer takes part? We
study this on one task, predicting what a Lua~5.1 program prints, with Qwen3-4B, under four series
registered before their runs and judged by an examiner program that holds an exam the learner never
reads (\cref{sec:method}).

The method that worked, S4, is pool-growing self-training (\cref{fig:cycle}). In each round the current
champion proposes programs about the language manual and predicts each one four times; the programs it
gets wrong at least once enter a pool, the interpreter supplies their outputs as labels, and a fresh
adapter is trained \emph{from the base} on the whole pool. The rounds share no parameters: only the pool
is passed on, so a ``generation'' is an acquisition round in which the current model synthesises queries
and its own disagreement over four samples selects them. The pool doubles each round (300, 600 and 1200
new programs).\cl{RS25,RS26}

\paragraph{What it shows, and what decides the rest.} Every S4 generation beat its predecessor on the
exam by the registered test, the share answered rising from 1.3\% to between 21.6\% and 27.1\%, and
accuracy on the learner's unselected dev sets rose from 40--55\% to 65--73\%
(\cref{fig:teaser}).\cl{RS28,RS46} Two things limit the reading. The third step does not survive the
examiner's template-clustered intervals in most lineages (\cref{sec:rsi-robust}), and each round trains
on more data than the last, so the gains may come from the amount of verified data rather than from an
improved model proposing better material. A registered matched-data control decides between these:
if generation~3 beats base-proposed data of the same size the improvement compounds and earns the word
recursive; if they are equivalent, verified data volume suffices; if the control is better, one round
is enough (\cref{sec:followups}).\cl{RS39,RS52} The earlier series, which failed, show what S4 needed
(\cref{sec:earlier}); the same loop on repository tasks and on a 27B agent showed no gain
(\cref{sec:elsewhere}).

\paragraph{Contributions.} (i)~A registered multi-round self-training procedure with a self-proposed,
oracle-labelled frontier that met a per-lineage criterion on a held-out exam, with its template-level
and procedure-level reading. (ii)~A learner/examiner separation specified fully enough to re-implement.
(iii)~A registry of every test of the four series, most of which did not meet their criterion.
(iv)~A claims ledger tying every statement to pinned code or a raw record (\cref{sec:repro}).
