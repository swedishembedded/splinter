\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

A model that poses its own problems can learn from them if something outside it supplies the correct
answers. Here that is an executable oracle: Qwen3-4B writes Lua~5.1 programs about the language manual,
and the interpreter says what each prints. This is environment-supervised learning: the model is trained
on the interpreter's targets for problems it gets wrong, including problems it never solves, not
reinforced on its own successes, and no stronger model or human answer takes part. Executable feedback
for self-improvement is not new;~\citep{haluptzok2022}\cl{CT24} our contribution is a registered procedure
that worked, the evidence that it did and what limits it, and the failure analysis of the series that
did not.

The procedure, S4, is pool-growing self-training (\cref{fig:hero}, detailed in \cref{fig:cycle}). In
each round the current champion proposes programs and predicts each four times; those it gets wrong at
least once join a pool labelled by the interpreter, and a fresh adapter is trained from the base on the
whole pool, which doubles each round (300, 900 and 2100 records).\cl{RS25,RS26} The rounds share no
parameters; the state that persists is the dataset.

\begin{center}
\fbox{\begin{minipage}{0.94\linewidth}\small
\textbf{Outcome of S4 (four lineages, three rounds; \cref{sec:rsi,sec:followups,sec:matched}).}\\[2pt]
\emph{Fresh exam} (2340 items, 78 templates, not selected by the base): 8.3\% $\to$ 8.7\% $\to$ 13.8\%
$\to$ 16.7\% answered; round 1 holds in no lineage, rounds 2 and 3 in all four by the sign test (round 3
in three by the clustered bound); the registered 3-of-4 criteria are not met.\cl{FU03,FU04}\\
\emph{Matched data} (one fit on 2100 base-proposed frontier programs): 14.6\%; the rounds' advantage of
2.1 points is inconclusive under the registered analysis.\cl{FU07}\\
\emph{Original exam} (889 items the base missed at selection): the registered criterion is met in 4 of 4
lineages; generation 3 holds in 1 or 2 of 4 under the clustered intervals.\cl{RS29,RS38}
\end{minipage}}
\end{center}

\paragraph{What it shows.} Accuracy grows with the verified frontier: on an exam drawn after the
fact, three rounds roughly doubled the share the base answered, and base-proposed frontier data scaled
the same way from 900 to 2100 records (\cref{fig:scaling}).\cl{FU04,FU07} Whether the rounds help beyond
the data they accumulate is not shown. We separate three senses of recursion: S4 has a recursive
\emph{structure}, since each round's data is proposed and selected by the previous round's model; a
\emph{benefit} of that recursion over matched data is not demonstrated; and an improving \emph{rate} of
improvement is not demonstrated (\cref{sec:matched}). The title states the condition we observed across
the registered runs and the scaling result, not a proven necessity: the growing frontier is what the
runs that improved share, while a fixed-size but better-selected frontier, more optimisation on fixed
data and other replay schedules were not tested. The first round's gain on the original exam was largely
the exam's selection on the base's failures (\cref{sec:followups}). The earlier series show what S4 needed
(\cref{sec:earlier}); the same loop on repository tasks and on a 27B agent showed no gain, in those
configurations (\cref{sec:elsewhere}).

\paragraph{Contributions.} (i)~A registered multi-round, environment-supervised training procedure on a
self-proposed frontier, examined on a fresh exam and against matched data. (ii)~A learner/examiner
separation specified fully enough to re-implement, with a reference implementation that reproduces the
published decisions. (iii)~A registry of every test of the four series and why most did not meet their
criterion. (iv)~A claims ledger tying every statement to pinned code or a raw record (\cref{sec:repro}).
