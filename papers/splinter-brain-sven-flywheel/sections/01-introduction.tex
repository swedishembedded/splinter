\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

A deployed local model does not know what its users' documents, tools and repositories say, and what
it is taught by hand is lost the next time it is replaced. A loop that lets the model learn from its
own verified experience, and releases a better version of itself, would remove that; the hard part is
knowing, without fooling oneself, whether a turn of the loop made the model better.

\paragraph{The question and the result.} We ask whether a model that teaches itself improves at every
one of several consecutive generations on items it never saw. We call a series \emph{self-taught} when
every generation's training material is proposed by the previous generation's champion and labelled
only by an executable oracle (here the Lua~5.1 interpreter): the policy writes the curriculum, never the
labels, and no stronger model and no human answer enters training. This is iterated self-training with
an oracle labeller, in the family of expert iteration and ReST-EM (\cref{sec:related}). Four series,
RSI1 to RSI4, were registered before their runs; from RSI2 on an examiner program separate from the
learner held the exam, excluded items that matched any trained program, and returned signed aggregate
verdicts (\cref{sec:rsi}).\cl{RS01,RS04,RS05} In RSI4, four lineages of Qwen3-4B, each generation
retrained from the base on the frontier of its predecessor's own proposals, met the registered
per-lineage criterion: every generation beat its predecessor by an exact sign test over the exam's
items, rising from 12 items right to between 192 and 241.\cl{RS28,RS29} The result has two qualifications
that the paper states with it. Under the examiner's own intervals clustered over the exam's 25 program
templates, the third generation's gain excludes zero in only one or two of the four lineages
(\cref{sec:rsi-robust}).\cl{RS38} And each generation trains on more data than the one before
(300, 900 and 2100 records), so the gains may come from the amount of verified data rather than from an
improved model proposing better material; a matched-data control and a re-examination on a freshly
drawn exam are registered and not yet run.\cl{RS26,RS39}

\paragraph{What did not show a gain.} The same loop, run in full on repository tasks, was registered
with an 8B student trained on a 27B teacher's verified trajectories: the confirmatory query did not show
the student better than its base (\cref{sec:coding-summary}).\cl{QUAL1} Two promoted generations of a
27B model trained on its own agent experience did not beat their base on one measurement
(\cref{sec:generations}).\cl{G2703,G2704} An exploratory run in which a 4B model learned from a raw
language manual gained on its own probes whoever wrote its training tasks
(\cref{sec:documentation}).\cl{SL23} The earlier RSI series located what the met criterion needed
(\cref{sec:rsi1,sec:rsi2,sec:rsi3}).

\paragraph{Scope.} The positive result is bounded: one task family (predicting the output of Lua~5.1
programs, the task of CRUXEval's output prediction~\citep{cruxeval2024}\cl{CT17} in another language),
one base model, three generations, LoRA training on the interpreter's labels, and an exam of items the
base did not answer when it was selected, generated from 25 templates, so it measures progress within
that distribution. It makes no claim of transfer to other tasks or models, of compounding, or of an
improvement on any public benchmark.

\paragraph{How claims are verified.} Every statement about the system or a measurement carries the id
of a row in a claims ledger (grey bracketed ids). A \textsc{structure} claim is a fact about the code,
a command line or a registration file, re-checked by \code{make verify} against the repositories at
pinned commits a fresh clone can reach; a \textsc{measured} claim is a number that
\code{scripts/build_data.py} reads from a raw record named, with its digest, in
\code{data/measurements.json}; a \textsc{computed} claim is derived from measured ones by a stated
formula; a \textsc{cited} claim points at a bibliography entry; a \textsc{planned} claim names a
registered experiment that has not run. A structure check shows that code exists, not that it behaves
accordingly, which only the experiments show (\cref{sec:repro}).

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(i)~A self-taught series that met a registered per-lineage criterion on a held-out exam, with the
examiner's protocol specified fully enough to re-implement, its cluster-robust reading, and the
failures of the earlier series that located what it needed (\cref{sec:rsi}).
(ii)~The learning loop and the division of labour among three projects that runs it
(\cref{sec:loop}, specified in \cref{app:system}).
(iii)~Registered tests of the loop that did not show a gain, reported as such: repository tasks with a
stronger teacher (\cref{sec:coding-summary}, \cref{app:coding}) and two generations of a 27B model
(\cref{sec:generations}), and an exploratory documentation run (\cref{sec:documentation}).
(iv)~A claims ledger that ties every statement to pinned code or a raw record (\cref{sec:repro}).
