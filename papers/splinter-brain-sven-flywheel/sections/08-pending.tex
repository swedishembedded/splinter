\section{The coding-task experiment: results}\label{sec:coding-results}

\paragraph{The question.} Can verified engineering-agent experience produce a sustained
improvement in completion of previously unseen repository tasks, under a controlled compute budget
and without unacceptable loss of earlier capability?\cl{PR09} The registered confirmatory query
did not show it: on the 46 qualification families the candidate registered before the query
reached a mean pass@1 of 23.0 against 20.0 for its base, winning 18 families, losing 12 and tying
16 (exact one-sided sign test $p=0.18$, not significant at the registered alpha). The same-seed
supervision contrast did separate: supervising only the decisive actions (file edits and writes)
against supervising every action, with corpus, recipe, seed and binary fixed, won 26 families and
lost 11 with 9 tied ($p=0.010$), and the six-decoding-seed development comparison on D46 had
given $p=0.040$ for the same candidate against its base.\cl{QUAL1,PROTO6} The sections below report
what was run, in order, with the pilots that shaped it.

\paragraph{What ran.} The experiments are registered in \code{prereg/coding-tasks.md}, which holds
the protocol of \cref{sec:protocol}. The headline's student is trained on verified trajectories
that the 27B teacher produced on development-partition families and is tested against the untrained
8B;\cl{PR15} the 27B model trained on its own verified trajectories (CE8) is a separate comparison
outside the headline.\cl{PR16} Run, in this order: the teacher's first pass over the eight
development sets (CE1), a pilot of the headline (CE4), two CE8 training runs, CE8-T with its
shuffled control and the G8 agreement check, development probes on D46, and finally the registered
confirmatory query. Registered experiments that were not run are named in \cref{sec:limitations}.
\Cref{tab:results} lists the paired outcomes that have a row in \code{data/results.csv}; every other
number is read from \code{data/measurements.json} through its claim.

\paragraph{Two findings of the first runs.} In a pilot under an earlier,
unbounded harness the untrained 8B model almost never edited a file, which is why the headline's
student trains on verified trajectories of the 27B teacher and why one bounded harness profile is
pinned for every arm. The first teacher run was withdrawn because its shell had no filesystem
confinement; of its 61 episodes, 29 were stopped by the repeated-answer bound and 5 by the tool-call
cap (the 34 conclusions labelled cancelled), and the solve report now names the bound.\cl{TJ01,TJ02}
Every workspace command has since been confined by the kernel and a call naming a path outside the
tree marks the episode exposed (\cref{sec:audit}).\cl{CN01}

\paragraph{CE4, the first confirmatory pilot: not shown.} The registered query could be run only on a subset (\cref{app:deviations}), under the protocol in force before the solve protocol of \cref{sec:protocol} (greedy decoding, one attempt per task): one task of each of the 23 families of the first qualification half, solved once by each arm with both served by Brain on the same build and tier. The adapter was trained on the verified, exposure-clean trajectories of the teacher's first pass, projected one tool call per turn. Of the 23 tasks, 21 are paired; the untrained 8B resolves one and the adapter none, so the sign test has a single discordant family against the adapter and does not support the claim; the comparison is a pilot by the registered rule and is reported as \emph{not shown}.\cl{M47} It is a weak test: with the untrained model near zero there is almost no room for a gain, and the adapter's attempts mostly end at the harness's repetition stop. The step count (47) is not the registered rule's (29 for the 114 training records the trainer reports), and the controls, the second qualification half, retention and the serve-agreement sample were not run.

\paragraph{CE8: trained, stopped by its guard, not evaluated.} The registered CE8 training ran
and stopped early at step 51: its training loss fell from 0.1476 at the first
step to 0.0221 at the last, and its best monitoring loss, 0.0839 at step 27 against 0.1101 for the
base, rose to 0.0867 by the last evaluation, so the registered guard calls it memorised.\cl{M48}
The one further run the registration allowed, on a one-pass budget, behaves the same way at a
smaller scale: best monitoring loss 0.0829 at step 30, 0.0832 at its last evaluation, training loss
0.1476 to 0.0282, memorised by the same rule.\cl{M49} Neither adapter is evaluated, and no claim
about CE8 follows from either. The guard reads a single step's training loss on two records and any
rise at the last evaluation, so it fires on noise of this size; the registration keeps it as
written, and the lower monitoring loss than the base's is a training-side quantity, not evidence of
a gain on any task.

\paragraph{CE8-T and its control: trained and evaluated on the pilot subset, no improvement.} CE8-T trained on
one pass over the 113 records the trainer reports (57 steps); its kept evaluation, step 48, has a monitoring
loss of 0.1738 against 0.1799 for the base and a training loss of 0.1331 there, and the training record's
verdict is not memorised, so CE8-T is a candidate for evaluation.\cl{M50} CE8-T-S, trained the same way on the
same 113 records and 57 steps, keeps step 57 at 0.2315 against 0.2635 for the base on its own shuffled
monitoring records, also not memorised.\cl{M51} Both are training-side quantities: the control's monitoring
records are shuffled too, so the two losses are not comparable, and neither is evidence of a gain on any task.

\paragraph{G8: the served adapter agrees with the trained one.} The registration puts G8 before any arm. On the
held-out records of the CE8-T training (33 records in 5 whole families, 354 examples, 68407 supervised
positions) every recorded assistant position is scored twice: by the in-process trainer's forward (bf16 base
with the adapter, no step) and by the evaluation server (Q8\_0 base with the adapter in its bank), and the same
pair is taken for the base. The mean absolute served-versus-in-process difference is 5.005e-02 for the base and
4.694e-02 with the adapter, 0.938 of the base's, so condition (i) holds; top-1 agreement is 97.37\% for the
base and 97.76\% with the adapter, a drop of -0.39 points, so (ii) holds; and the served adapter's mean
log-probability, -0.162778, is above the served base's, -0.181931, so (iii) holds and the verdict is
PASS.\cl{M54} The served adapter is therefore the adapter that was trained, and the evaluation that follows is
of the trained artifact.

\paragraph{The pilot evaluation: no held-out improvement.} Both candidates were then solved on the pilot subset S1 (23 tasks, one per family of the first
qualification half) under the solve protocol of \cref{sec:protocol} with thinking off, one attempt per task,
on one frozen build and one server process that offered the base and both adapters. CE8-T resolves 7 of the
23 tasks and the base 9; of the 13 paired families the adapter wins 3, loses 4 and ties 6, and the one-sided
sign test gives $p=0.7734375$.\cl{M52} The shuffled control resolves 7 and, over 16 paired families, wins 2,
loses 6 and ties 8 ($p=0.96484375$).\cl{M53} Neither comparison reaches the registered alpha, and the rule
that labels an underpowered comparison a pilot applies (13 and 16 non-tied families against the 25 the rule
requires): the outcome of both arms is reported as \emph{no held-out improvement}, and no confirmatory query
follows for CE8-T. The adapter does change behaviour on many of the same tasks, but not in one direction, and
the trained adapter is not distinguishable from its shuffled-instruction control in size or direction. The
next levers are a decision-level view of the same trajectories and more verified teacher data, not a longer
run of this recipe.

\paragraph{CE8-T-u1b: decision-level supervision, the same outcome.} The pilot's result motivated a third
candidate that changes one variable of the data rather than the recipe: CE8-T-u1b trains on the unpacked
\code{sft-step} view of the same experiences, one record per admitted action with its own context, instead of
one packed record per trajectory. The trainer reports 2490 records, and the run takes 1200 steps at a held-out
monitoring share of 0.05; its kept evaluation, step 960, has a monitoring loss of 0.1676 against 0.1835 for
the base and a training loss of 0.1359 there, and the guard calls it not memorised.\cl{M55} Solved on the same
pilot subset under the same protocol it resolves 6 of the 23 tasks where the base resolves 9; of the 13 paired
families it wins 1, loses 4 and ties 8, and the one-sided sign test gives $p=0.96875$, with 6 attempts exposed
against 7 for the base.\cl{M56} Supervising decisions rather than whole trajectories does not rescue the
comparison: the candidate is at or below the base and below its packed predecessor on this subset. Both
candidates are development probes, not registered comparisons.

\paragraph{What these pilots can and cannot show.} Two measurements bracket what the pilot numbers mean.
First, the attempts differ in shape. The median episode is 32 steps for the base, 13 for CE8-T, 12 for its
shuffled-instruction control and 16 for the decision-level candidate; the arms make 782, 474, 392 and 632 tool
calls, and their solve conclusions report 11, 10, 9 and 3 successes, while the longest episodes still run to 58,
60, 61 and 52 steps.\cl{M58} The control matches the trained adapter, so what fine-tuning on these records moves
is when a turn ends, not what a trajectory solves; the decision-level candidate sits between the two, with more
steps than the packed candidates and the most repetition stops. Second, the seed floor: solved twice on the same
23 tasks with two registered protocol seeds, the base itself pairs 13 families with 1 win, 3 losses and 9 ties,
$p=0.9375$, which is statistically indistinguishable from either adapter's comparison.\cl{M57} A pilot of this
size therefore shows no detectable difference, not a demonstrated loss; the registered confirmatory query on 46
families, reported below, is the measurement that could separate them. The training side points the same way: every trajectory
longer than the 16384-token block was removed before training (130 of the 262 successful attempts of the eight
development sets, and 1148 of 3837 for the decision-level view), so the records the adapters were trained on are
the short half of the teacher's successes, and their median record carries about 1650 supervised tokens.\cl{M59}

\paragraph{Development probes on D46: the supervision ablation and recovery.} On 2026-10-25, on node n184 (two GH200 cards), two series of development probes were run on D46 (46 tasks, 46 families, one task per family, greedy decoding) with one rebuilt training binary (SHA-256 \code{1d366e597bb504bc222304ebbe07ef0165c442f503091340e740004475a120ab}); they are reported as pilots and are \emph{not} the registered confirmatory query, and their outcomes are not used as evidence for it. The first series holds the corpus (769 records, \code{blake3:646692b2}), the recipe (rank 16, alpha 32, $5\times10^{-5}$, 120 steps, packed-bf16 base), the training seed and the binary fixed and changes only the supervision: every admitted action, or \code{--only-actions edit\_file,write\_file} (\code{blake3:92252209}). At seed 13 the decisive-action adapter verifies 30 of 46 tasks with 1 exposed, the all-action adapter 13 with 3 exposed; over 42 paired families the decisive arm wins 16 and loses 1 (exact one-sided sign test $p=0.0001373291015625$), and the all-action arm is a regression below its own base (13 against 21, 4W-11L-20T, $p=0.982421875$).\cl{D46A,D46B} At seed 1 the same contrast keeps its sign but is not significant (23 against 18, both with 4 exposed; 38 paired, 8W-4L-26T, $p=0.19384765625$); the two seeds share the 46 families, so this is one strong same-seed demonstration plus a sign-consistent second seed, not two independent tests.\cl{D46C} The behaviour is seed-stable: the decisive adapters of seeds 1, 7 and 13 edit a file in 35, 36 and 39 of their 46 attempts and end 1, 0 and 2 in the repetition stop, where the all-action adapter edits 22 and aborts 10 and the base edits 24 and aborts 13.\cl{D46D} The same-seed design removes the rebuild confound that earlier comparisons carried: with corpus, recipe, seed and executable fixed, changing only the supervision changes the outcome.

The second series recovers the all-action adapter \code{win1}, damaged to 12/46, on the base's own verified D46 successes (\code{blake3:df000e8f}, 14 records) at $5\times10^{-5}$. Six steps (303 s of training) verify 23 of 46 with 2 exposed against the base's 21 (36 paired, 4W-6L-26T, $p=0.828125$); 18 steps verify 20 with 5 exposed (34 paired, 6W-8L-20T, $p=0.78802490234375$, measured 2026-10-24); 36 steps (815 s) verify 28 with 7 exposed (31 paired, 10W-5L-16T, $p=0.15087890625$); and the undamaged control, the decisive seed-1 adapter trained for 18 steps on the same corpus, verifies 24 with 10 exposed (30 paired, 7W-2L-21T, $p=0.08984375$).\cl{RCVA,RCVB,RCVC,RCVD} The first six steps -- about five minutes -- therefore do most of the restoration, and longer continuation on the benchmark's own families buys a few more tasks while raising the exposed count: the corpus is a labelled rehearsal of the scored families, not a transfer measurement and not free gain. The doses are one seed and share their start, corpus and families, so they are not independent tests, and no comparison reaches the registered alpha.

\paragraph{Six decoding seeds on D46.} The development benchmark was re-solved under the protocol's own sampling at six registered decoding seeds (the three of the earlier pilots and three drawn for this run) by the base, the decisive-action adapter of training seed 13 and its all-action control, on two GPUs. Mean pass@1: base 19.67, decisive 24.5, all-actions 19.83. Per family, decisive wins 22, loses 11 and ties 13 against the base (exact one-sided sign test $p=0.0401$), 22W-11L-13T against the all-action control ($p=0.0401$), and the control 17W-15L-14T against the base ($p=0.430$): the supervision separates from both controls, while the two controls do not separate from each other.\cl{PROTO6} This is a development probe on D46 (46 families, not the qualification set) and no candidate is registered. It is the powered comparison that justifies registering the candidate and running the registered query, not a result of that query.

\paragraph{The registered confirmatory query: not shown.} The candidate registered before the run - the decisive-action adapter of training seed 13 - was solved against its base and its all-action control on the frozen qualification set (46 families) at the protocol's three registered decoding seeds and recommended sampling. Mean pass@1 was 23.0 for the candidate, 20.0 for its base and 16.33 for the control, with exposed attempts on 10, 35 and 22 of the 138 assigned respectively. Per family the candidate won 18, lost 12 and tied 16 against its base: the exact one-sided sign test reads $p=0.1808$ with a family-bootstrap 95\,\% interval of $[-0.065, +0.188]$ for the mean-fraction difference. At the registered alpha of 0.05 this is \emph{not shown}: the candidate is not demonstrated better than its base on the qualification set, and the paper does not report equivalence. The same-seed supervision contrast does separate: against the all-action control - same corpus, recipe, seed and binary, differing only in the supervision - the candidate wins 26 families and loses 11 ($p=0.0100$, mean fraction +0.145, interval $[+0.007, +0.275]$), while the control against the base is 16W-11L-19T ($p=0.221$). That contrast is a secondary analysis, not the registered decision. The development read on D46 had reached 22W-11L-13T at $p=0.0401$; it did not replicate at the registered level, and the registration's own rule for a development gain with no qualification gain (sign test $p>0.2$, reported as memorisation) is not met either, since $p=0.1808$ - the registered result is a null whose direction matches the development read, next to a causal supervision effect that the control isolates.\cl{QUAL1}

\paragraph{Generation 2: rehearsal protected nothing at this budget.} The decisive seed-1 champion (D46 23/46, S1 16/23) continued on S1's verified successes (25 records) for 120 steps at $5\times10^{-5}$, seed 1; the arms differ only by \code{--rehearsal} of the decisive corpus at 0.25. On D46 the no-rehearsal arm verifies 28 of 46 with 7 exposed (32 paired, 8W-4L-20T, $p=0.19384765625$) and the rehearsal arm 19 with 5 exposed (34 paired, 4W-7L-23T, $p=0.88671875$); the direct same-start contrast pairs 37 families with 3 rehearsal wins and 13 losses ($p=0.9979095458984375$ with rehearsal as the candidate, 13W-3L the other way), so rehearsal cost nine tasks relative to the same update without it.\cl{G2A,G2B,G2C} On S1 they verify 12 (5 exposed) and 11 (1 exposed) against the base's 8 (13 paired, 2W-2L, $p=0.6875$; 15 paired, 1W-0L, $p=0.5$), so acquisition over the champion's 16/23 is not demonstrated.\cl{G2D} At this budget the new data did not make the model forget D46 (the no-rehearsal arm added five tasks over its start), so there was nothing for rehearsal to protect and it cost tasks instead. These are one-seed greedy runs on the development benchmark, not the registered query, and the run-to-run variation in the limitations applies to them.\cl{REP1}
