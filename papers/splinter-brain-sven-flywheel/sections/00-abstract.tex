\begin{abstract}
A model can learn from problems it poses itself when an executable oracle supplies the answers.
Qwen3-4B proposed Lua~5.1 programs; those it predicted wrong at least once in four samples joined a pool
labelled by the interpreter, and each round trained a fresh adapter from the base on the pool, which
doubled each round. Registered beforehand and judged by a separate examiner, four lineages raised
accuracy on a freshly drawn 2340-item exam from 8.3\% to 16.7\% in three rounds: the first round gained
nothing, the second and third gained in every lineage. One fit on the same amount of base-proposed
frontier data reached 14.6\%, so the rounds' 2.1-point advantage is inconclusive under the registered
analysis, while base-proposed data scaled from 900 to 2100 records. Accuracy grew with the verified
frontier; that the model proposing it must improve is not shown. Three earlier registered series did not
meet their criteria, and the same loop showed no gain on repository tasks or for a 27B agent in those
configurations.
\end{abstract}
