\begin{abstract}
A model can learn from problems it poses itself when an executable oracle supplies the answers.
Qwen3-4B proposed Lua~5.1 programs; those it predicted wrong at least once in four samples joined a pool
labelled by the interpreter, each round adding twice as many programs as the last (300, 900, 2100 in
total), and each round trained a fresh adapter from the base on the pool. On the original exam the
procedure met its registered criterion, an item-level test of each adapter against its predecessor. On a
2340-item exam drawn afterwards, accuracy rose from 8.3\% to 16.7\%; that follow-up's registered criteria
were not met because round 1 gained nothing, while rounds 2 and 3 added 5.0 and 2.9 points on average,
both intervals above zero. One fit on the same amount of base-proposed frontier data reached 14.6\%, so
the rounds' 2.1-point advantage is inconclusive. Three earlier
registered series met no per-lineage criterion, and the same loop showed no gain on repository tasks or
for a 27B agent in those configurations.
\end{abstract}
