\begin{abstract}
A local model that is meant to keep learning from the documents, tools and repositories
it works with needs a loop from source to release that can be inspected stage by stage.
We describe the self-learning flywheel built by three cooperating projects: Splinter decides
what is learned, Brain trains and serves, and Sven runs the agents. The loop takes a source,
has a model propose tasks that code admits or refuses for named reasons, attempts them
through an agent runtime, keeps what typed verifiers confirm, projects verified experience
into training data, trains a LoRA adapter or consolidates into the weights, and releases the
result only if four checks over unseen material pass, with immutable releases and rollback.
Every statement about the system carries an id in a claims ledger and is re-checked against
the three repositories at pinned commits (121 structure claims, 232 checks); every number
comes from a raw run record. Four small experiments exercise the loop; all are pilots, and the first wave of two of them
failed and exposed four defects, repaired here with regression tests. A task funnel on documentation
pages refused 82 of 144 proposals in the first wave and 75 of 137 on the repaired binary because the
generator did not name the task's subject, in 45 of the 75 cases by omitting it from the question;
only the markup-only class (18 of 82) was a defect of the rule. The registered two-generation
turn on invented facts with a 0.6B policy did \emph{not} reach its success criterion: its candidates
learned 13 of 30 facts (none at base) and passed the improvement, retention and serve checks, and the
gate refused each of them on the anchor check alone, which allows no lost answer of 40 questions, so no
release, no retention across releases and no rollback to a first release was obtained. A post hoc run with
a 1.7B policy was released by the gate and kept the anchor. Qualification runs of the 27B model on one 95~GiB
card fit an NF4 base at 8192 tokens in about 36~GiB and a bf16 base at 16384 tokens in about 78~GiB.
We list what was not measured.
\end{abstract}
