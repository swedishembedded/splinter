\begin{abstract}
Can a small model improve over several rounds by training on problems it proposes itself, with labels
only from an executable oracle? Qwen3-4B proposed Lua~5.1 programs; those it mispredicted at least once
in four samples joined a growing pool labelled by the interpreter, and each round trained a fresh
adapter from the base on the whole pool. In four seeds, registered beforehand and judged by a separate
examiner on 889 held-out items the base had missed, every round beat its predecessor by the registered
item-level test, raising accuracy from 1.3\% to 21.6--27.1\%. Two caveats: under intervals clustered
over the exam's 25 templates the third round holds in only one or two seeds, and the pool grows each
round, so verified data volume and a better proposer are not yet separated; a registered matched-data
control and a fresh exam will decide. Three earlier registered series failed, and the same loop showed
no gain on repository tasks or for a 27B agent.
\end{abstract}
