\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

Each subsection reports one step of \cref{sec:method}: what it was tested on, the measurement with its interval, and where the
evidence is. Every number is regenerated from the files under \texttt{data/} by \texttt{scripts/paper\_data.py}
(\cref{app:reproduction}).

\subsection{The growing pool of interpreter-labelled programs (Lua)}\label{sec:r-pool}

\paragraph{Tested on.} Four independent lineages of Qwen3-4B, four generations each, retrained from the base at every
generation on the frontier pool (1000 to 8000 records), each lineage's 16 candidates handed to the examiner. The examiner's
sealed exam has 2040 items in 136 program families; 870 items come from 58 families never used in any exam. No item was
excluded for overlap with a candidate's training programs (median token-set Jaccard similarity to the nearest training program
0.50). Direct answers, thinking off.

\paragraph{Measured.} Generation~4 answers 13.24\% of the items against the base's 5.05\%: $+8.19$ points, 95\% interval
$[+5.12, +11.52]$ (bootstrap over program families and lineages). On the 58 never-examined families the gain is $+3.25$
$[+0.66, +7.10]$. The first two generations each improve on their predecessor (\cref{tab:lua}, \cref{fig:generations}). All
four lineages move together: generation~4's shares are 12.3\% to 14.0\%.

\subsection{Practice tasks authored from the Vim manual}\label{sec:r-authoring}

\paragraph{Tested on.} The 263 items of the manual, three authoring variants each, with the 27B author.

\paragraph{Measured.} Of 6705 candidate tasks, 1030 were admitted (682 with one step, 252 with two, 71 with three, 25 with
four). The most frequent refusals: a step that changes nothing (1822), a review that found the result does not match the
request (1209), Vim's \texttt{E37} (500), a duplicate (454). The base student passes 10.0\% of its attempts on the admitted
tasks and the teacher 81.1\% (\cref{tab:vim-attempts}); the funnel is in \cref{fig:vim-funnel}. Evidence:
\nolinkurl{material/funnel.json} and the material runs' manifests in \nolinkurl{swedishembedded/source-to-transfer-evidence};
tasks in \nolinkurl{swedishembedded/vim-headless-mastery-manual}.

\subsection{Oracle admission}\label{sec:r-admission}

\paragraph{Tested on.} The 1030 section-by-section and 467 outcome-first tasks, the reference replayed once in the task
image.

\paragraph{Measured.} The oracle solved 1026 of 1030 and 467 of 467. The four failures were an admission defect, not task
defects: admission had read Vim's output in text mode (turning CR~LF into LF) and run Vim under a locale the task image lacks
(so a euro sign was recorded as a different byte), and the reviewer had approved each. After the fix (bench \texttt{f73ae591}),
replaying the 1030 references gives the same expected bytes for the 1026 the oracle solved and catches the four. A task
without an oracle solution is never attempted, so no trajectory on the four entered training.

\subsection{Outcome-first authoring}\label{sec:r-outcome}

\paragraph{Tested on.} The same 263 items. 771 outcome-first proposals; 66 refused (file names that are not plain names,
carriage returns, duplicates); the teacher reached 562 of the 705 end states within 4 attempts; 523 of those replay to the end
state; 56 requests that quote most of the target or the solution were refused; 467 tasks remain.

\paragraph{Measured.} Trained with the same selection rule, the outcome-first tasks' trajectories transfer more to the sealed
exams than the section-by-section tasks': 42 exam passes of 1200 against 22, 25 exams solved at least once against 10, from
1100 training records of 348 tasks against 3359 of 857. Per exam: 21 won, 5 lost (two-sided sign test $p=0.0025$), pass-rate
difference $+0.017$ $[+0.003, +0.031]$. This is a secondary comparison of the registration, and it does not isolate the
authoring mode: under the shared 800-step budget with early stopping on validation loss, the outcome-first arm kept step 520
in every seed (about 1.9 passes over its records) while the all-verified arm ran all 800 steps (about 0.95 passes); and the
outcome-first tasks are harder (student pass rate 0.031 against 0.100, teacher 0.722 against 0.811), closer to the exams.
Evidence: \nolinkurl{decision/secondary-eval-exam.json}.

\subsection{Verified trajectories into adapters, on sealed exams}\label{sec:r-adapters}

\paragraph{Tested on.} 15 adapters (5 arms $\times$ 3 seeds), each examined on the 100 sealed exams with 4 attempts per exam
against a base run in the same evaluation stage. Infrastructure ended at most 11 of a stage's 800 attempts (1.4\%), all on the
base side, within the registered 2\% bound.

\paragraph{Measured.} Every arm of the registered family beats the base under Holm and beats the shuffled-request control
(\cref{tab:vim-results}). The control, trained on the same records and format with deranged requests, stays at the base's
zero in all three seeds, so the gain comes from requests paired with the actions that satisfy them, not from exposure to the
tool's format. Every seed of every other arm passes at least two exam attempts (\cref{fig:vim-seeds}). The registered claim
holds for this generation: trajectories on tasks authored from the manual alone teach the 4B student skills it applies to exams
it never saw. The absolute level is low: the outcome-first arm passes 3.5\% of exam attempts (difference from its paired base
$+0.035$ $[+0.021, +0.051]$), the all-verified and productive-actions arms 1.8\%, one-per-task 0.8\%; the teacher passes 63\%
of first attempts. Evidence: \nolinkurl{decision/decision-eval-exam.json}; adapters in
\nolinkurl{swedishembedded/vim-manual-transfer-adapters}.
