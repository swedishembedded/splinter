\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

Each subsection reports one step of \cref{sec:method}: what it was tested on, the measurement with its interval, and where the
evidence is. Every number is regenerated from the files under \texttt{data/} by \texttt{scripts/paper\_data.py}
(\cref{app:artifacts}).

\subsection{The growing pool of interpreter-labelled programs (Lua)}\label{sec:r-pool}

\paragraph{Tested on.} Four independent lineages of Qwen3-4B, four generations each, retrained from the base at every
generation on the frontier pool (1000 to 8000 records), each lineage's 16 candidates handed to the examiner. The examiner's
sealed exam has 2040 items in 136 program families; 870 items come from 58 families never used in any exam. No item was
excluded for overlap with a candidate's training programs (median token-set Jaccard similarity to the nearest training program
0.50). Direct answers, thinking off.

\paragraph{Measured.} Generation~4 answers 13.24\% of the items against the base's 5.05\%: $+8.19$ points, 95\% interval
$[+5.12, +11.52]$ (bootstrap over program families and lineages). On the 58 never-examined families the gain is $+3.25$
$[+0.66, +7.10]$. The first two generations each improve on their predecessor (\cref{tab:lua}, \cref{fig:generations}). All
four lineages move together: generation~4's shares are 12.3\% to 14.0\%.

\subsection{Oracle admission}\label{sec:r-admission}

\paragraph{Tested on.} The 1030 section-by-section and 467 outcome-first tasks, the reference replayed once in the task
image.

\paragraph{Measured.} The oracle solved 1026 of 1030 and 467 of 467. The four failures were an admission defect, not task
defects: admission had read Vim's output in text mode (turning CR~LF into LF) and run Vim under a locale the task image lacks
(so a euro sign was recorded as a different byte), and the reviewer had approved each. After the fix (bench \texttt{f73ae591}),
replaying the 1030 references gives the same expected bytes for the 1026 the oracle solved and catches the four. A task
without an oracle solution is never attempted, so no trajectory on the four entered training.

\subsection{Outcome-first authoring}\label{sec:r-outcome}

\paragraph{Tested on.} The same 263 items. 771 outcome-first proposals; 66 refused (file names that are not plain names,
carriage returns, duplicates); the teacher reached 562 of the 705 end states within 4 attempts; 523 of those replay to the end
state; 56 requests that quote most of the target or the solution were refused; 467 tasks remain.

\paragraph{Measured.} Trained with the same selection rule, the outcome-first tasks' trajectories transfer more to the sealed
exams than the section-by-section tasks': 42 exam passes of 1200 against 22, 25 exams solved at least once against 10, from
1100 training records of 348 tasks against 3359 of 857. Per exam: 21 won, 5 lost (two-sided sign test $p=0.0025$), pass-rate
difference $+0.017$ $[+0.003, +0.031]$. The comparison (secondary in the registration) is between the authoring modes as run: the
outcome-first arm also saw each record about twice (step 520 of the shared 800-step budget, kept by validation loss in every
seed) where the all-verified arm saw each about once (all 800 steps), and its tasks are harder (student pass rate 0.031
against 0.100, teacher 0.722 against 0.811), closer to the exams.
Evidence: \nolinkurl{decision/secondary-eval-exam.json}.

\subsection{Verified trajectories into adapters, on sealed exams}\label{sec:r-adapters}

\paragraph{Tested on.} 15 adapters (5 arms $\times$ 3 seeds), each examined on the 100 sealed exams with 4 attempts per exam
against a base run in the same evaluation stage. Infrastructure ended at most 11 of a stage's 800 attempts (1.4\%), all on the
base side, within the registered 2\% bound.

\paragraph{Measured.} Every arm of the registered family beats the base under Holm and beats the shuffled-request control
(\cref{tab:vim-results}). The control, trained on the same records and format with deranged requests, stays at the base's
zero in all three seeds, so the gain comes from requests paired with the actions that satisfy them, not from exposure to the
tool's format. Every seed of every other arm passes at least two exam attempts (\cref{fig:vim-seeds}). The registered claim
holds for this generation: trajectories on tasks authored from the manual alone teach the 4B student skills it applies to exams
it never saw. The outcome-first arm passes 3.5\% of exam attempts (difference from its paired base
$+0.035$ $[+0.021, +0.051]$), the all-verified and productive-actions arms 1.8\%, one-per-task 0.8\%; the teacher passes 63\%
of first attempts. Evidence: \nolinkurl{decision/decision-eval-exam.json}; adapters in
\nolinkurl{swedishembedded/vim-manual-transfer-adapters}.

\subsection{The iterated loop per model size}\label{sec:r-loop}

\paragraph{Tested on.} Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B, each from its own base. Generation~2's tasks follow a rule that combines a
section's idea with ideas of at least two other chapters: 573 were admitted and the oracle solved 572; the 4B's generation-1
champion passed 0.223 of its 4576 counted attempts on them and solved 280 of the 572. A Qwen3.8-27B learner was not started: one 27B
generation would have ended at about 08:30 on 2026-11-27, leaving no time for a second before the compute lease ended, and by
the registered rule a size is run only if its generations can finish.

\paragraph{Measured.} Both bases pass none of the exams: Qwen3-4B none of \BaseCalFourAttempts{} counted attempts and
Qwen3-8B none of \BaseCalEightAttempts{} in a calibration of 12 attempts per exam. Qwen3-4B, generation~1:
\LoopFourGOnePasses{} of \LoopFourGOneAttempts{} exam attempts against its paired base's \LoopFourGOneBasePasses{} of
\LoopFourGOneBaseAttempts{}; \LoopFourGOneWins{} exams won, \LoopFourGOneLosses{} lost (one-sided sign test
$p=\LoopFourGOneP$); \LoopFourGOneSolved{} exams solved at least once. It was promoted on its own held-out tasks, where it
passes \LoopFourGOneHeldout{} of attempts against the base's \LoopFourGOneHeldoutBase. Qwen3-8B, generation~1 (trained from the
8B base on the outcome-first tasks with the 8B's own verified attempts and the teacher's, seed~3, step 520 kept): \LoopEightGOnePasses{}
of \LoopEightGOneAttempts{} exam attempts against its paired base's \LoopEightGOneBasePasses{} of \LoopEightGOneBaseAttempts{};
\LoopEightGOneWins{} exams won, \LoopEightGOneLosses{} lost (one-sided sign test, $p$ = \LoopEightGOneP); \LoopEightGOneSolved{} exams solved. The gain
replicates over two more training seeds of the same generation: seed~1 passes \LoopEightRepOnePasses{} of 400 against its base's
0 of \LoopEightRepOneBaseAttempts{} (\LoopEightRepOneWins{} exams won, none lost, \LoopEightRepOneSolved{} solved), seed~2
\LoopEightRepTwoPasses{} of 400 against 0 of \LoopEightRepTwoBaseAttempts{} (\LoopEightRepTwoWins{} won, none lost,
\LoopEightRepTwoSolved{} solved); on the held-out authored tasks they pass 53 and 54 of 204 attempts against their bases' 0 of
203 and 0 of 200. In all three seeds the base arm left more slots uncounted than the registered 2\% bound (19, 21 and 15 of
400; the two replicates' were requests the server rejected, whose cause could not be determined afterwards), and the stages
were not repeated (\cref{app:deviations}). The release gate of seeds 1 and 2 was inconclusive, so neither was promoted, and the
8B curve continues on seed~3, whose held-out-task evaluation and release are running.

Qwen3-4B, generation~2 (trained from the base on generation 1's and generation 2's records, measured against the generation-1
champion in the same stages, every slot counted): \GTwoExam{} of 400 exam attempts against the champion's \GTwoChampExam{},
\GTwoExamWins{} exams won and \GTwoExamLosses{} lost (one-sided $p=\GTwoExamP$), difference \GTwoExamDiff; on its own held-out
tasks \GTwoHeld{} of \GTwoHeldAttempts{} against \GTwoChampHeld{} (\GTwoHeldWins{}/\GTwoHeldLosses{}, $p=\GTwoHeldP$); on
generation~1's held-out tasks, the forgetting gate, \GTwoForget{} of \GTwoForgetAttempts{} against \GTwoChampForget{}
(\GTwoForgetWins{}/\GTwoForgetLosses{}, $p=\GTwoForgetP$). Generation~2 did not beat generation~1 at 4B, and it lost nothing
either; the release was inconclusive and the candidate was not promoted. The champion's own exam count here (\GTwoChampExam{}
of 400) and when generation~1 was first measured (\LoopFourGOnePasses{} of 400) are two samples of the same adapter.

Qwen3-8B, generation~2 (trained from the 8B base on generations 1 and 2, against the 8B generation-1 champion, every slot
counted) shows the same pattern: \GTwoEightExam{} of 400 exam attempts against \GTwoEightChampExam{}, \GTwoEightExamWins{}
exams won and \GTwoEightExamLosses{} lost ($p=\GTwoEightExamP$), difference \GTwoEightExamDiff; own held-out tasks
\GTwoEightHeld{} of \GTwoEightHeldAttempts{} against \GTwoEightChampHeld{} (\GTwoEightHeldWins{}/\GTwoEightHeldLosses{},
$p=\GTwoEightHeldP$); forgetting gate \GTwoEightForget{} of \GTwoEightForgetAttempts{} against \GTwoEightChampForget{}
(\GTwoEightForgetWins{}/\GTwoEightForgetLosses{}, $p=\GTwoEightForgetP$). It was not promoted either. By the loop's rule the
champion is the latest promoted release, so generation~3 of each size started from its generation-1 champion (4B at 07:00, 8B
at 09:08; 528 new tasks shared by both sizes, records of generations 1 to 3, released against generation~1), and generation~4
of the 4B (tasks one step harder: 6 to 14 interacting changes over at least four other chapters, every job on several files)
is being authored. No human step chose anything.

\paragraph{Continuity as registered.} Per size, each generation's champion is compared with the base and with the previous
champion, Holm over the generations of the size. Generation~1 beats the base at both sizes (4B \LoopFourGOneWins{} exams won,
\LoopFourGOneLosses{} lost, $p=\LoopFourGOneP$; 8B \LoopEightGOneWins{}/\LoopEightGOneLosses{}, $p$ = \LoopEightGOneP).
Generation~2 does not beat generation~1 at either size (4B \GTwoExamWins{}/\GTwoExamLosses{}, $p=\GTwoExamP$; 8B
\GTwoEightExamWins{}/\GTwoEightExamLosses{}, $p=\GTwoEightExamP$), so each size's champion is still generation~1 and the
criterion that every generation improves on its predecessor is not met at generation~2. Generations 3 and 4 are pending; the
trend test of the pass rate over generations is computed once a size has two promoted generations. Evidence: \nolinkurl{loop/loop.json}.

\paragraph{What limited generation~2.} Two independent analyses of the generation-2 runs at both sizes measured the
following.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Effect size.} Pooled over both measurements of the 4B generation-1 champion, it passes 28 of 800 exam attempts
  (3.5\%) against generation~2's 13 of 400 (3.25\%): no exam gain at 4B. At 8B, generation~1 over three seeds and four
  measurements passes 97 of 1600 (6.1\%) against generation~2's 30 of 400 (7.5\%). On generation~1's held-out tasks
  generation~2 gains about 1.18-fold at both sizes (4B 0.240 against 0.203, 8B 0.294 against 0.249). The variance between
  training seeds is within binomial noise (8B exams 22, 24, 28 of 400).
\item \emph{Training budget.} The recipe capped training at 800 LoRA steps while each generation trains from the base on a
  growing dataset (new records plus all earlier generations' as replay); the learning rate follows a cosine to a tenth of its
  peak after a warmup of a twentieth of the steps. At the selected step generation~1 had seen its records 1.89 (4B) and 1.92
  (8B) times, generation~2 0.97 times at both sizes, still improving at the cap. From generation~3 the recipe holds the epochs
  constant (\cref{app:deviations}). This restores generation~1's exposure in records; generation~1 stopped at step 520 of an
  800-step cosine with the rate still at about 35\% of its peak, so a full cosine over the same epochs gives each record
  about 26\% less cumulative learning rate, and the patience of 160 steps is 0.58 epoch at generation~1 and 0.13 at
  generation~3.
\item \emph{Training material.} 45\% of the 8B generation~2's new records (42\% of its supervised characters) are the
  champion's own (6--8\% at generation~1), and 64\% of those are on tasks the champion already passes at least 5 of 8 times;
  replay is a plain union, so the teacher's share of the gradient rose only about 12\%. Validation uses only the new
  generation's records, 41\% of whose characters are the champion's (3\% at generation~1), so checkpoint selection rewards
  imitating the champion.
\item \emph{What it learned.} Generation~2 made the 8B commit sooner; it did not edit better (\cref{tab:calls}). The champion's
  records explain it: 34\% are exactly two calls and 48\% contain no error, against 8\% and 13\% of the teacher's. Median
  completion tokens per exam trial fell from about 1530 to about 1000. Repeated identical calls (same call, same result) are
  42\% of the champion's exam calls and 62\% of generation~2's; 5.3\% of the champion's supervised calls carry sven's repeat
  note (teacher 0.6\%).
\item \emph{Tasks.} Generation~2's tasks are as large and as hard as generation~1's (workspace 11.9 against 13.0 lines, teacher
  pass rate 0.72 against 0.68); 15.7\% meet the authoring prompt's 15-to-80-line specification, because the prompt's own
  worked example has files of 12 and 5 lines. Output was not length-limited (median 1.7k of 16k tokens).
\item \emph{Release gate.} The forgetting evaluation had no declared purpose and defaulted to a target, so promotion required
  two superiority tests at once; the probability of promoting a true 1.25-fold step is about 0.19 at 4 attempts per task,
  about 0.85 at 16. The 8B generation~2 was held back with its forgetting evaluation reading $+0.069$ and its own held-out test
  inconclusive.
\item \emph{Exam resolution.} With 4 attempts per exam the sign test has 13 to 19 discordant exams; its power to detect a
  1.25- to 1.5-fold improvement is 0.12 to 0.44 (0.40 to 0.94 at 40 attempts). The course's practice tier (228 tasks, base
  rate 0.47) has power 0.98 for a 1.15-fold effect at 4 attempts and is the most sensitive measurement available.
\item \emph{Failure modes and time.} A failing exam attempt declares the job done with wrong content in 65--78\% of failures,
  repeats an identical call in 62--66\% of failing trials, meets Ex errors (mostly E486, pattern not found), and stalls on a
  single request until the 480-second limit in 5--8\%. Trials ended by wall clock or budget are 6\% of trials, use 27--31\% of
  trial time and pass none; sven's own limit of tool rounds defaults to 200, above the registered 60 turns.
\end{itemize}
Measured and excluded as causes: a prompt or mode mismatch (the system prompts are byte-identical), truncation, duplicate
records, context exhaustion, budget differences, forgetting, leakage, and uncounted slots.

\paragraph{Three generations after the fixes (Qwen3-8B).} The defects above were fixed in place in the loop's recipes
(bench \texttt{d971e5df}, splinter \texttt{578cfec9b}) and the three generations were run again at 8B from the authoring run,
unattended, from 17:16 to 23:58 on 2026-11-27 (\cref{app:deviations}). The changes: training and validation data are the
teacher's verified attempts; the champion's are replay, one per task drawn at random, at half the draws; from generation~2
the LoRA continues the champion at a learning rate of $10^{-4}$ with patience 8; the budget is 1.9 epochs at every
generation; sven's tool rounds are capped at the registered 60 turns; the champion's attempts are 4 per task; and a release
needs both its target (its own held-out tasks) and retention (every earlier generation's held-out tasks, margin 0.05) with at
most 2\% unpaired slots. Every generation was promoted (\cref{fig:rerun}). Each comparison is paired, 4 attempts per task, one-sided sign test
over tasks:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Generation 1 against the base.} Own held-out tasks 85 of 224 against 9 (79 won, 3 lost, $p=1.9\times10^{-20}$,
  difference $+0.339$ [$+0.237$, $+0.442$]); exams 25 of 400 against 0 of 391 (25 won, 0 lost, $p=3.0\times10^{-8}$).
\item \emph{Generation 2 against generation 1.} Own held-out tasks 55 of 188 against 40 (29 won, 14 lost, $p=0.016$,
  $+0.080$ [$+0.011$, $+0.154$]); generation~1's held-out tasks 92 of 224 against 86 (29/23, $p=0.24$); exams 30 of 400
  against 30 (17/17, $p=0.57$).
\item \emph{Generation 3 against generation 2.} Own held-out tasks 59 of 196 against 45 (25 won, 11 lost, $p=0.014$);
  generation~2's held-out tasks 49 of 188 against 54 (14/19, within the retention margin); generation~1's 109 of 224 against
  105 (18/14, $p=0.30$); exams 32 of 400 against 32 (18/18, $p=0.57$).
\end{itemize}
So each of the three generations improves on its predecessor on tasks it was not trained on, authored from the manual by its
own generation's rule, and none forgets an earlier generation's tasks beyond the margin. On the 100 hand-written exams the
registered primary tests (each generation against its champion in the same stage) find no step: 17/17 and 18/18. Generation~1
against the base reads 25 of 400 against 0, but that evaluation left 9 of the base's 400 slots uncounted (2.25\%), and by the
registration an evaluation above the 2\% bound is reported and excluded from claims. Exam transfer is therefore not claimed for
this rerun; generations 2 and 3 pass 30 and 32 of 400, as many as the champion each was measured against. Every one of the 9 slots was a
trial in which the base model repeated itself inside a tool call's arguments until sven ended the run; the worker recorded the
hang-up as HTTP 499 and the classifier took it for an endpoint's refusal, retried it and left it uncounted (fixed in splinter
\texttt{27372f64b}, after these runs; the live runs kept one rule throughout). Counting all 9 as base wins would give 25 won and
9 lost, $p=0.005$; the claim stays excluded as registered.

\paragraph{Three generations after the fixes (Qwen3-4B).} The same recipes, bound to Qwen3-4B, ran after the 8B's three

\cref{app:deviations}).
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Generation 1 against the base.} Own held-out tasks 64 of 224 against 6 of 222 (62 won, 4 lost,
  $p=1.0\times10^{-14}$); exams 11 of 400 against 0 of 397 (11 won, 0 lost, $p=0.00049$), with 3 of the base's slots
  uncounted, within the 2\% bound. Promoted.
\item \emph{Generation 2 against generation 1.} Own held-out tasks 35 of 188 against 31 (17/13, $p=0.29$); generation~1's
  held-out tasks 76 of 224 against 68 (27/19, $p=0.15$); exams 11 of 400 against 12 (10/11, $p=0.67$). Not promoted.
\item \emph{Generation 3 against generation 1}, still the champion: own held-out tasks 41 of 196 against 27 (24 won, 10 lost,
  $p=0.012$); generation~2's held-out tasks 30 of 188 against 31 (11/12); generation~1's 83 of 224 against 68 (28/13,
  $p=0.014$); exams 13 of 400 against 12 (11/10, $p=0.50$). Promoted.
\end{itemize}
At 4B the exams show one claimable step of transfer, generation~1 over the base; no later generation beats its champion on
them. On authored tasks generation~2 did not beat generation~1, and generation~3 did, both on its own tasks and on
generation~1's.

\paragraph{Training-seed replicates of the steps (secondary).} Each step after generation~1 was repeated with only its newest
LoRA's training seed changed, registered before any replicate result (\nolinkurl{loop/seed-replicates.json}): 4 seeds of each
8B step (generation~3's fourth did not finish its evaluations before the lease ended and is not counted, so 3) and 5 of each
4B step. Pooled over seeds (tasks won and lost summed, one-sided sign test), against the same champion:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{8B generation~2.} Own held-out tasks 99 won, 53 lost ($p=0.0001$); generation~1's held-out tasks 115/84
  ($p=0.017$); exams 63/64 ($p=0.57$). Every seed gains on its own tasks (49 to 55 of 188 against 40).
\item \emph{8B generation~3.} Own held-out tasks 64/35 ($p=0.002$); generation~2's 61/59, generation~1's 52/48; exams 66/57
  ($p=0.24$).
\item \emph{4B generation~2.} Own held-out tasks 76/65 ($p=0.20$; one seed of five loses, 25 against 31); generation~1's
  held-out tasks 154/84 ($p<10^{-5}$); exams 58/46 ($p=0.14$).
\item \emph{4B generation~3} (against generation~1). Own held-out tasks 102/51 ($p=2\times10^{-5}$); generation~2's 88/56
  ($p=0.005$); generation~1's 172/74 ($p<10^{-9}$); exams 56/44 ($p=0.14$).
\end{itemize}
Over seeds, three of the four steps improve on held-out authored tasks and none loses an earlier generation's tasks; the
4B's second step improves on the first generation's tasks rather than its own. No step improves on the hand-written exams
(the strongest is 66 won to 57 lost); the single-seed results above are not a seed's accident in either direction.

\subsection{Retention on hand-written practice tasks}\label{sec:r-retention}

\paragraph{Tested on.} The course's \RetainTasks{} hand-written practice tasks (168 exercises and 60 composites of the
public course dataset's training side), which no arm trained on and which test skills the base already partly has. Three
generation-1-era 4B candidates (the 4B loop's generation~1, and one seed each of the productive-actions and one-per-task arms)
and the 8B's generation~1 (all three training seeds), each against its base, 4 attempts per task, with the plan of the held-out authored
tasks. This is an exploratory measurement; the registered retention check of the loop compares each generation with its predecessor (\cref{sec:m-loop}).

\paragraph{Measured.} No candidate is worse than the base overall; each gains (\cref{fig:retention}, \cref{tab:retention}). The
4B's generation~1 passes \RetainPasses{} of \RetainAttempts{} attempts against the base's \RetainBasePasses{} of
\RetainBaseAttempts{}, winning \RetainWins{} tasks and losing \RetainLosses{}; the 8B's generation~1 gains in all three training seeds
(\RetainEightMinWins{} to \RetainEightMaxWins{} tasks won, \RetainEightMinLosses{} to \RetainEightMaxLosses{} lost; seed~1
passes \RetainEightPasses{} of 912 against its base's \RetainEightBasePasses{} of \RetainEightBaseAttempts{}). Almost all of the gain is on the exercises, while on the composites all stay near zero. A candidate
does lose on some tasks (10 to 17 of 228), so the absence of regression is net, not task by task. The 8B base arms left 19, 9 and 15 of
912 slots uncounted (seeds 1, 2, 3). Evidence: \nolinkurl{retention/} of the evidence dataset (per-task outcomes; these tasks are public),
recipes in bench \nolinkurl{recipes/vim-manual/transfer-arms/retention-*.yaml}.
