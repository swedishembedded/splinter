\begin{abstract}
We measure whether a loop of authoring tasks, attempting them and training on the verified attempts improves a local model
generation after generation, with no human-written training tasks or labels and every generation judged on exams no stage
reads. The registered estimand is the exam pass rate per generation, per model size. On 100 hand-written Vim exams, the first
generation of Qwen3-4B, trained on verified trajectories of tasks that a 27B model authored from Vim's user manual, passes 17 of
400 attempts against its base's 0 of 393 (12 exams won, 0 lost, one-sided sign test $p=0.00024$), and the first generation
of Qwen3-8B 22 to 28 of 400 in each of three training seeds against bases that pass none (16 to 20 exams won, none lost). At both sizes the second generation did not beat the first (4B: 13 against 11 of 400 exam attempts, 8 exams won and
5 lost, $p=0.29$; 8B: 30 against 23, 12 won and 7 lost, $p=0.18$), lost nothing on the first generation's held-out tasks, and
was not promoted. With the recipe defects this exposed fixed in place, three 8B generations ran unattended in under seven
hours and each was promoted: each beats its predecessor on its own held-out authored tasks (generation~1 85 against 9 of 224
attempts; generation~2 55 against 40 of 188, $p=0.016$; generation~3 59 against 45 of 196, $p=0.014$) without forgetting an
earlier generation's; on the exams generations 2 and 3 do not beat their champions (30 against 30, 32 against 32 of 400),
and generation~1's 25 of 400 against the base's 0 is excluded from claims because 2.25\% of the base's slots went
uncounted (bound 2\%). At 4B the fixed loop's generation~1 beats the base on the exams (11 of 400 against 0, 11 exams won and
none lost, $p=0.0005$), generation~2 is not promoted, and generation~3 beats generation~1 on authored tasks (41 against 27 of
196, $p=0.012$) but not on the exams (13 against 12). A 27B learner is not run. The steps that lead there were each measured: an
executable environment as the only judge of correctness (a Lua~5.1 interpreter, in a first family, raised a 4B model's share
of a 2040-item sealed exam from 5.1\% to 13.2\% over four generations, +8.2 points [+5.1, +11.5], +3.3 [+0.7, +7.1] on program
families no exam had used); oracle admission that replays every reference in the task image; outcome-first authoring (start and
end files first, the request last), whose trajectories pass 42 of 1200 exam attempts against 22 for section-by-section tasks on
a third of the records; and two controls. Trained on the same records with shuffled requests, the model passes no exam, so the
gain comes from the pairing of request and action; trained on the 4B's own verified attempts alone, it passes none of 1166, so
generation~1's gain comes from the 27B teacher's trajectories. On 228 hand-written practice tasks no candidate is worse
than its base overall (the 4B's generation~1: 378 of 912 attempts against 138; the 8B's, in each of three seeds, 408 to 463 against 134 to 139). Against the operational definition of continuous
self-improvement stated in the introduction, the first generation holds at both sizes; after the fixes three 8B
generations each improve within the authored task distribution, at 4B two of three steps do, and the only claimable exam step is
the 4B's first.
\end{abstract}
