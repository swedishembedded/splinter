\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\paragraph{The run.} All four lineages completed four generations; every candidate passed the learner's gate and was handed to
the examiner (16 candidates). On the learner's own development tasks the champions rose from 0.34--0.53 (the base) to
0.74--0.81 after generation~4. Four dated deviations, all before any candidate was examined, are recorded beside the
registration: the gate's planning target (0.08 to 0.09), the proposer state moved to memory when the disk's file quota filled,
one lineage's last generation moved to a freed card, and the fourth generation's proposal budget raised after one lineage
spent it with 3706 of its 4000 frontier programs found. None changes a proposal, a training record or a gate decision.

\paragraph{Exam G.} The examiner's exam G has 2040 items in 136 program families; 870 items come from 58 families never used
in any exam. No item was excluded for overlap with any candidate's training programs; the median similarity of an item to its
nearest training program (token-set Jaccard) is 0.50, and one item reaches 0.9. The base answers 5.05\% of the items; two
independent servings of the base differ on one item each way. Table~\ref{tab:rsi5} gives the registered comparisons: mean share
of items right over the four lineages, differences with 95\% intervals from a bootstrap over templates and lineages.

\paragraph{Criteria.} \textbf{G1 (every generation better than its predecessor) is not met}: steps 1 and 2 hold, steps 3 and 4
do not. \textbf{G2 (generation 4 at least five points above the base) is met}: $+8.2$ points, lower bound $+5.1$.
\textbf{G3 (gain on families never used in any exam) is met}: $+3.3$ points, lower bound $+0.7$.

\paragraph{What this shows.} The loop of Splinter, Sven and Brain more than doubles the model's direct-answer accuracy on a
fresh sealed exam, and the gain reaches program families that no exam had used. It does not improve at every generation:
each doubling of the verified pool buys less (5.1, 1.6, 1.2, 0.3 points), and generations 3 and 4 are indistinguishable from
their predecessors. The learner's own gate advanced every generation, including on development leads that were not
significant, so it did not detect the plateau. On items the base already answered, every comparison involving generation 1
loses ($-37$ points on that small stratum), the same regression toward the mean the companion paper observed on exam F.

\paragraph{A leading indicator in the learner's own records (descriptive).} A frontier pre-training effort that decomposed
self-improvement into smaller loops found that an internal signal rose well before downstream scores plateaued, and that a
flat headline score can hide growing headroom~\cite{cai2026nosecondrun}. RSI5's learner journals hold two such signals, read
without any exam data. The frontier yield (the share of measured proposals a champion gets wrong at least once) fell from
0.73--0.91 in generation~1 to 0.39--0.54 in generation~2 and stayed there, while step~2 still improved. The proposer's novelty
(new programs admitted per proposal request) held at 0.45--0.94 through generation~3 and collapsed in generation~4 in every
lineage (0.42, 0.27, 0.56, 0.34 for g0--g3), the generation whose step is flat: the champion increasingly re-proposed programs it
had already proposed. With four lineages this is a hypothesis for the next registered run, not a finding: a controller that
watches proposer novelty could stop, or change its source of programs, before spending a generation that does not pay.
