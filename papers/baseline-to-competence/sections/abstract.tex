\begin{abstract}
A small language model that has a text editor as its only tool fails tasks a larger model solves: after an editor error it often
repeats the failed command. We describe and measure a process that takes 0.6B, 4B and 8B models
toward competence at editing files with headless Vim, graded on 100 held-out tasks. The process collects verified teacher
trajectories, adds the student's own verified solutions and a teacher's continuations of the student's failures from the
step where they went wrong, admits each record only as a new solution of its task, and trains a LoRA adapter. The base
models solve none of the counted exam slots, even with four reruns of every failed task. The round-one 4B adapter solves
\examFourFullFive{} and the 8B adapter \examEightFullFive{} within five attempts; the 0.6B adapter solves
\examZeroFullFive{} of 100, so we treat that size as not reaching the exams with this tool and data. A second round of recovery
data was followed by \examEightRtwoFive{} at the 8B (\pEightRtwo{} against the round-one adapter, not confirmed) and
\examFourRtwoFive{} at the 4B, and a policy-gradient stage by \examEightPgFive{} at the 8B (one run). A reward that penalises
looping did not reduce the share of attempts ended by the loop watchdog, which stays near 30\% on the exams. Every count is
derived from released run records by the scripts of the appendix; rerunning the exams gives counts within the spread reported
in the limits.
\end{abstract}
