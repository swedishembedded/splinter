\paragraph{Records.} A record is one complete trajectory under the system prompt and tool schema the model is served,
every assistant turn supervised, in the form the model's chat template renders. Only trajectories the grader passed
become records. The first records are a teacher's verified solutions of the practice tasks, almost all by the 27B model.

\paragraph{The student's own solutions.} The trained adapter attempts every practice and composite task four times at
the evaluation sampling settings (the 4B and 8B a second time at temperature 1.0 for variety). Each verified pass is a candidate record. These records are on-policy, so they teach
the model what it can already do more reliably, and they reach tasks the teacher records cover with other tool-call
sequences.

\paragraph{Recovery data.} A failed attempt is the training signal a pass cannot give. For a task the adapter failed, the
teacher reads the failed attempt and names the step after which it went wrong, keeping the failing call and its result so
that the continuation sees the error the student did not recover from, and writes one or two sentences of guidance that
state no solution. A fresh sandbox is seeded with the kept prefix and the teacher continues. A verified continuation is
stored as one conversation in which the guidance turn is removed: the student sees its own mistake followed by the
corrective call, as it would at inference. Tasks that the adapter solved in some attempts and failed in others are
continued from their failed attempts too, so recovery data covers partial as well as total failures.

\paragraph{Integration and provenance.} A candidate record enters the set only as a new solution of its task. Its
fingerprint is the sequence of tool calls (tool name and whitespace-normalised arguments, ignoring the free-text intent
argument), and a record whose fingerprint the task already has is dropped; at most twelve records per task are kept (sixteen in the second round). The
split is by whole task: roughly one tenth of tasks new to the set are held out for validation, and a task keeps its side.
Every record carries the model that produced it (\cref{tab:data}).

\paragraph{Supervised training.} A LoRA adapter of rank 16 and alpha 32 is trained for up to 600 optimiser steps of four records each, learning
rate $2\times10^{-4}$, on a bf16 base. The held-out loss is measured every 40 steps, training stops after four
evaluations without improvement, and the step with the best held-out loss is kept.

\paragraph{Policy gradient with a loop penalty.} Starting from the trained adapter, a clipped policy-gradient stage
uses the adapter's own graded attempts at the practice tasks (eight per task, temperature 1.0), grouped by task. The reward of an
attempt is one for a pass and zero for a failure, less a penalty for each of two behaviours read from the attempt's files:
a full pass's reward when the loop watchdog ended it, and half of one scaled by the share of its calls after a Vim error that
repeated the failed call. Advantages are normalised within the task's group \citep{grpo}, so a looping attempt is pushed down
against its siblings whether or not either passed. The clip band is wider above than below and the loss is summed over
tokens \citep{dapo}; the KL term is taken against the adapter the run starts from, which is also the policy that sampled
the attempts, so the importance ratio needs no recorded log-probabilities. Training rows are bounded at 16\,384 tokens so that
long attempts, which are the looping ones, stay in the data.
