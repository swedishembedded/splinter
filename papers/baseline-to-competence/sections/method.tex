\Cref{fig:pipeline} shows the process. A training set of verified records trains an adapter; the adapter's attempts
at the training tasks yield new records, both from its passes and, through the teacher, from its failures; and the
enlarged set trains the next adapter. A policy-gradient stage can follow the supervised one.

\paragraph{Records.} A record is one complete trajectory, rendered by the model's chat template under the system prompt
and tool schema the model is served with, in which every assistant turn is supervised. Only trajectories that the grader
passed become records. The first records are verified solutions of the training tasks, almost all by the 27B teacher; the set also holds
\dataExternalTrain{} solutions by a third model, deepseek-v4.1-flash, of tasks the teacher did not solve.

\paragraph{The student's own solutions.} A trained adapter attempts every training task four times at the evaluation
sampling settings, and the 4B and 8B adapters four more times at temperature 1.0 for variety. Each passing attempt is a
candidate record. These records are sampled from the student's own policy, and those that are admitted are by
construction tool-call sequences the set did not yet contain for their task.

\paragraph{Recovery data.} A passing attempt cannot show how to continue after an error from which the student did not
recover. For that, the teacher reads a failed attempt, names the step after which it went wrong, and writes one or two
sentences of guidance that state no solution. The attempt is cut after the failing call and its result, a fresh sandbox
is brought to the state that this prefix produced, and the teacher continues from there. A continuation that the grader
passes is stored as one conversation with the guidance turn removed, so that the record shows the student's own mistake
followed directly by the corrective call, as the student would meet it at inference. Failed attempts of tasks that the
adapter solved in other attempts are continued as well, so that recovery data covers partial as well as total failures.

\paragraph{Admission and provenance.} A candidate record enters the set only as a new solution of its task. The
fingerprint of a record is its sequence of tool calls (tool name and whitespace-normalised arguments, without the
free-text intent argument); a candidate whose fingerprint its task already has is dropped, and at most twelve records
are kept per task (sixteen in the second round). The split into training and validation is by whole task: about one
tenth of the tasks new to the set are held out for validation, and a task never changes side. Every record names the
model that produced it (\cref{tab:data}). We call the set before the student's solutions and the recovery data are
added the \emph{round-one set} (\integrationTrainBefore{} training records) and the set after their admission the
\emph{integrated set} (\integrationTrainAfter{} training and \integrationValidationAfter{} validation records); the
\emph{round-one adapter} and the \emph{round-two adapter} of a model are trained on the former and the latter.

\paragraph{Supervised training.} A LoRA adapter \citep{lora} of rank 16 and alpha 32 is trained on a frozen bf16 base
with a learning rate of $2\times10^{-4}$ for at most 600 optimiser steps of four records each on the round-one set; the runs on
the integrated set ran to step 800 (\cref{fig:loss}). The loss on the validation records is evaluated every 40 steps, training stops after four evaluations without
improvement, and the step with the lowest validation loss is kept. The 27B student arm of \cref{sec:task} is an adapter for the teacher model trained on the published teacher
records.

\paragraph{Policy gradient with a loop penalty.} The second stage starts from a trained adapter and optimises a clipped
policy-gradient objective \citep{ppo} on that adapter's own graded attempts at the training tasks, eight per task at
temperature 1.0. The reward of an attempt is
\begin{equation}
r = \mathbf{1}[\text{pass}] - \lambda_{\text{loop}}\,\mathbf{1}[\text{loop-stopped}] - \lambda_{\text{rep}}\,\rho,
\label{eq:reward}
\end{equation}
where an attempt is \emph{loop-stopped} when the watchdog ended it and $\rho$ is the share of its calls after a Vim
error that repeated the failed call. We use $\lambda_{\text{loop}}=\optimiserFourLoopWeight$ and
$\lambda_{\text{rep}}=\optimiserFourRepeatWeight$, and in one variant twice both. Advantages are normalised within the
group of attempts at the same task \citep{grpo}, so a looping attempt is pushed down relative to the other attempts at
its task whether or not any of them passed. As in DAPO \citep{dapo}, the clip band is wider above than below and the
loss is summed over tokens. The attempts are sampled from the adapter the stage starts from, so the KL term is taken
against that adapter and the importance ratio needs no recorded log-probabilities. Training rows are bounded at
16\,384 tokens, which keeps the long attempts, and the looping attempts are among the longest.
