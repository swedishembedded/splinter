\section{The task and its measurement}\label{sec:task}
\paragraph{Agent and tool.} The agent receives the request and one tool, which runs Ex commands in a fresh headless
Vim on the task's files and returns Vim's output, including its error messages. An attempt ends when the agent stops,
after 60 turns, after 16\,384 generated tokens, or after eight minutes of wall-clock time. A \emph{loop watchdog} also
ends an attempt in which the model issues the same tool call six times or the same reasoning three times; we call such
an attempt \emph{loop-stopped}. The models run with their reasoning block disabled and are instructed to state their
intent in one or two sentences before every call. Sampling uses temperature 0.7, top-p 0.8 and top-k 20 for every model
and arm.

\paragraph{Tasks.} There are three families of tasks, each task a container with a request, starting files and a hidden
grading script. The 168 \emph{practice} tasks each teach one idea (append a line, change inside braces, record a macro).
The 60 \emph{composite} tasks combine ideas from several chapters of the course on material of their own. Together
these 228 are the \emph{training tasks}: everything that is trained on comes from them. The 100 \emph{exams} are
outcome-only requests that combine several ideas on files that no training task shows, grouped by material (C source,
configuration, logs, low-level code, markup, multi-file edits, prose, Python, Rust and Go, shell and build files, and
others). The composite tasks were written by an independent agent that never read the exams, and an audit found no
request identical to an exam's and a maximum token overlap of 0.29 with any exam. Exam tasks and exam trajectories are
never read for training, and exam results are not used to choose a checkpoint.

\paragraph{Protocol.} An \emph{arm} is one model, with or without an adapter, measured on the 100 exams. Every task is
attempted once, and every task whose first attempt failed is attempted four more times. \emph{Pass@1} is the number of
tasks solved by the first attempt; \emph{solved within $k$} is the number of tasks with a pass among their first $k$
attempts, and \emph{solved within five} is the main figure. Both arms of a comparison get the same chances. A
\emph{slot} is one scheduled attempt of one task by one arm. A slot \emph{counts} when its attempt ended with a grade;
an attempt lost to an infrastructure failure is retried, and a slot that still has no grade after the retries is
\emph{uncounted} and is not a pass.

\paragraph{Statistics.} Intervals on a count of solved tasks are 95\% Wilson score intervals \citep{wilson} for a
proportion of 100 tasks. A candidate arm is compared with a reference arm by an exact one-sided sign test paired over
tasks: among the tasks that counted in both arms, a task solved within five by the candidate alone is
a win and one solved by the reference alone is a loss, ties are dropped, and $p$ is the probability of at least the
observed number of wins under a fair coin. The reference of a round-one adapter is its base model; the reference of a
later stage is the round-one adapter, measured again in the same run as the candidate. We test at $\alpha=0.05$ and do
not correct for the number of comparisons. A candidate arm is valid only if every slot counts and at most 2\% of its
attempts end in an infrastructure timeout (the wall clock expired before the model answered once). The recipes were
fixed before their exams were run; \cref{sec:discussion} states the one recipe that was changed after an exam result
and the one arm that misses the validity rule by one slot.

\paragraph{Models.} The students are Qwen3-0.6B, Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B \citep{qwen3}, each trained as a LoRA adapter
\citep{lora} on a frozen base. The teacher is Qwen3.8-27B-FP8. One GH200 GPU (96\,GB) serves and trains each student,
and the teacher shares a card with the student whose failures it continues. The 27B model also appears once as a
student: an adapter trained for it on the published teacher records is measured by the same protocol, with the attempt
instead of the task as the unit of the paired test.
