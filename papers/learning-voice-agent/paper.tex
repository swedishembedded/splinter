\begin{abstract}
Most of what a user corrects in a working agent is not how it speaks. They say that a fact it stated is wrong, that it must
never do something again, or how a piece of work is done here. We treat such feedback as data. A turn, spoken or typed, is read into
a typed \emph{teaching}---a fact, a rule or a procedure---by a conservative reader that a model can assist; the agent restates what it
understood, keeps it only on a plain yes, tells the model what it has been taught on the turns it bears on, lets a corrected fact replace
the old one, and takes a teaching back on request. On facts about an invented company that no model can know, a new session of an
8B model holding the ledger answered \TeachFactTaught{} questions as taught against \TeachFactUntaught{} without it, kept corrected
facts corrected, and left forgotten facts and unrelated answers alone; two rules taught at once were not both obeyed. A model reader found
\ReadModelFound{} teachings in authored natural phrasings where plain phrase matching found \ReadPlainFound{}. The same layer carries
lessons about how to speak (pronunciation, imitation, preference), whose training machinery is shown to learn a planted reward but not yet
naturalness. All voices are synthetic theatrical portrayals. Every claim is listed with its status in \cref{tab:claims}; the utterances
are authored by us and fed mostly as text, so the results measure the mechanism and not yet how people phrase corrections.
\end{abstract}
