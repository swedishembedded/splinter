\begin{abstract}
Most of what a user corrects in a working agent is not how it speaks. They say that a fact it stated is wrong, that it must never do something again, or how a piece of work is done here. We
treat such feedback as data. A turn, spoken or typed, is read into a typed \emph{teaching}---a fact, a rule or a procedure---by a conservative reader that a model can assist; the agent restates what it understood,
keeps it only on a plain yes, tells the model what it has been taught on the turns it bears on, lets a corrected fact replace the old one, and starts the conversation over when a teaching is taken back. On facts about an invented company that no
model can know, a new session of an 8B model holding the ledger answered \TeachFactTaught{} questions as taught against \TeachFactUntaught{} without it, kept corrected facts corrected, and did not return forgotten facts or disturb unrelated answers; the procedure gate was not met for lack of asks, and two rules taught at once were not both obeyed. A model reader found \ReadModelFound{} teachings in authored natural phrasings where plain phrase matching found \ReadPlainFound{}, with one false reading in 38 non-teachings, and
\SpokenPassed{} scripted spoken conversations passed end to end through recognition. The same layer carries lessons about how to speak; its group-relative optimiser over the synthesizer lowers frames per word on a planted reward below two baselines in one seed, which shows that it moves a policy and not that it improves naturalness. All voices are synthetic theatrical portrayals. The utterances are authored by us, so the results measure the mechanism and not yet how people phrase corrections.
\end{abstract}
