\begin{abstract}
A user who talks to a voice agent often wants to correct how it speaks: ``pronounce Jefferson as
Jeff-er-son'', ``talk as follows: \ldots'', ``that sounded wrong''. We treat such feedback as data.
A spoken or typed correction is read into a typed \emph{lesson} with a stated learning objective
(a pronunciation lexicon, an in-context demonstration, a supervised example, or a preference),
kept with its provenance and the label of the synthetic voice it applies to, applied at synthesis
time where no training is needed, and queued for training where it is. Training from preference
uses group-relative policy optimisation over the synthesizer's codec policy, rewarded by naturalness
and word error rate together, anchored to the original voice, and released only through a gate
against baselines on a frozen exam. This draft fixes the formalism, the pre-registered experiments
and the reproduction pipeline, and reports what is already measured: first-sound latency of the
speaking agent. Every other claim is listed with its status in \cref{tab:claims}; none is made
until its test has run.
\end{abstract}
