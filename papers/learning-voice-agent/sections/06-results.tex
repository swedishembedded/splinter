\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Teaching an agent}

\paragraph{Facts, rules and procedures (T2).}
The test split of \cref{sec:evaluation} was run once under \code{prereg/teaching-test.md} (run \code{teaching-test-20261010}; \TeachScenarios{} scenarios, \TeachAsks{} asks; Qwen3-8B, non-thinking, CUDA). \Cref{tab:hypotheses} gives each pre-registered hypothesis and its verdict, and \cref{tab:teaching} the
rates by kind. Untaught, the model answered none of the questions about the invented facts (\TeachFactUntaught{}); with the ledger of a session that had been taught, in a new session, it answered \TeachFactTaught{} as taught. A fact that was taught and then corrected was answered as
corrected and never as first taught, a fact taught and then forgotten was not told, unrelated questions kept their answers, and sixteen facts taught in one session were each recalled. The last two kinds are leak and disturbance checks: both arms should pass, and they do.

One pre-registered gate is not met: the procedure kind has only \TeachProcedureAsks{} asks, so its interval (lower bound \TeachProcedureLow{}) cannot reach 0.80 even when every ask passes, and a claim for procedures needs more scenarios. Rules (\TeachRulesAsks{} asks), a fact that must be used and two rules at once had no gate and are reported as measured. Two rules
taught together were not both obeyed: every reply ended with ``over'' but none used ``captain'', which the same rule taught alone had produced in every reply. The gates on facts are met over asks; the registration names the scenario as the unit, and over the twelve fact scenarios the lower bound is \TeachFactScenarioLow{}, below 0.80. The agent restated every teaching utterance of the set for confirmation
(\TeachUnderstood{} of \TeachTeachings{}), but these were written in the forms the plain reader knows, so that number tests the protocol and not the reader.

\paragraph{Other model sizes (T2).}
Under \code{prereg/teaching-models.md} the unchanged test split was run once each on Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-14B (runs \code{teaching-test-4b-20261010}, \code{teaching-test-14b-20261010}); \cref{tab:models} shows the pass counts beside the 8B model's. The three sizes agree on every kind except two rules taught together, which the 14B model obeyed in all four asks and the smaller two in none.
That is one scenario, so it says only that the failure is not shared by all sizes. The H1 and H2 verdicts of \cref{tab:hypotheses} hold for all three models.

\paragraph{Taking a teaching back in the conversation that heard it (T2).}
Under \code{prereg/teaching-retract.md}, five scenarios (one rule with three asks, four facts with two) were asked in the same session after ``Forget that''; all \RetractPassed{} asks passed (lower bound \RetractLow{}; run \code{teaching-retract-20261010}). The check is the absence of the taught content, the control arm passes it
trivially, and only one scenario is a rule, so this shows that the taught content did not come back and not that anything was learned. It holds because the conversation starts over: with the conversation kept, a rule taken back was still followed because the model followed its own earlier replies (development scenarios, six of six asks failed).

\paragraph{Teaching by voice (T4).}
Three scripted conversations were run end to end under \code{prereg/teaching-spoken.md}: the user's turns rendered in one synthetic voice and recognised, the agent hearing only the recognised text, its replies spoken. All \SpokenPassed{} met their pre-registered conditions. In the first, a spoken fact was restated, confirmed and recalled; the recogniser heard ``atlas seven'' as ``\SpokenHeardServer{}'', the agent restated what it heard, the user confirmed, and the wrong value is what was recalled. In the second, a rule was
restated, confirmed, obeyed, taken back by ``Forget that'', and not obeyed after. In the third, a procedure was restated and told, though ``quartz'' was heard as ``court's'' and the restatement and the answer carry that. These are three conversations in one voice under a persona prompt, a demonstration that the path works and not a rate. Their first runs found
that the pronunciation desk took ``Forget that'' before the ledger did (it is now routed to the kind taught last), and the retraction behaviour above.

\paragraph{Reading natural phrasings (T1, T3).}
\Cref{tab:reading} scores the readers on the test split of the authored utterances (\(80\) of \(121\): 42 teachings and 38 non-teachings; run \code{teaching-read-20261010}). The plain reader never fired on a non-teaching (0 of 38) and found \ReadPlainFound{} teachings, so the gate of T1 is met by it; the model reader found \ReadModelFound{} and fired on \ReadModelFired{} non-teachings
(``Please don't be late'' was read as a rule), so T1's gate is not met by the model reader, and one fire in 38 does not exclude a false-fire rate of several per cent. Asking the model only when the plain reader finds nothing gives the same numbers, because the plain reader never fires on a non-teaching. Test utterances only the model found include ``no no it's Monday not Friday'', ``the invoices go out on Fridays'',
``don't act until I say go'' and ``the way we onboard a customer is to send the welcome mail, schedule the kickoff, and create their workspace''. The model missed ``the deploy window is Thursday at 14:00 by the way'' and read ``when I say the usual, I mean the weekly report'' as a fact. A development false fire of the plain reader (``Next time I will bring coffee'') was fixed before the test split was scored.
The utterances are written by the implementer, so these numbers measure coverage of phrasings we thought of, not of the ones users produce.

\subsection{How the agent speaks}

\paragraph{A tail of silence before recognition.}
Of \TakesN{} takes in one pinned voice (run \code{takes-pilot-20261009}), the streaming transducer returned a transcript differing from the text for
\FirstRecogniserRaw{} when the take ended with the speech and for \FirstRecogniserTailed{} when 0.6\,s of silence followed it; the fixed-window
recogniser differed for \SecondRecogniserRaw{} and \SecondRecogniserTailed{}. Both recognisers, given the tail, heard within the word-error bound
\TakesAgreed{} of \TakesN{} takes; they disagreed on \TakesContested{}. A take is therefore recognised with a tail of silence, and a pronunciation judgement needs two recognisers that agree; twenty takes are a screen, not a result.

\paragraph{A planted reward (S1).}
Before any claim about naturalness can be tested, the loop that trains the synthesizer from scores of its own takes has to move a policy. With a reward that has a known answer (fewer codec frames per word, i.e.\ speaking faster), \GateUpdates{} updates over groups of \GateGroup{} takes moved the held-out sentences from \GateFramesBefore{} to \GateFramesAfter{} frames per word
while the recognised word error rate of the held-out takes went from \GateWerBefore{} to \GateWerAfter{}; the KL estimate to the original policy reached \GateKlLast{} at the last update (run \code{rl-speed-gate-20261010}; eight held-out sentences, three takes each, one run and one seed). \Cref{tab:methods} puts the same gate under the two baselines, with the same sentences, takes per update, seed and number of updates.
GRPO ended lowest in frames per word, the pairwise step (DPO) \(0.12\) above it and imitating the best take of each group \(0.73\) above it; the first difference is within what one seed on eight sentences can resolve, and GRPO's word error rate rose to nearly twice its start while the other two did not. The reward here is frames per word alone, not the reward of \cref{sec:formalism}.
The final KL estimates (GRPO 0.57, best-of-n 0.30) are not comparable at equal drift, and the pairwise step does not estimate one. This shows the optimiser moves a policy and that a reward is exploited, which is why the reward of \cref{sec:formalism} carries a word-error term; it says nothing about naturalness.

\paragraph{Reading directives about how to speak (S2).}
The plain readers of directives about how to speak (\cref{sec:formalism}) were run on the test split of \ExtractTestSize{} authored utterances, written with their gold label before the readers were run and not changed after (run \code{extraction-rules-20261010}): 101 directives and 29 non-directives, among them near-misses such as ``Say hello to the delegates'' and ``What does pronounce mean?''.
\Cref{tab:extraction} shows that no reader fired on a non-directive (\ExtractFalsePositives{} false positives in 29, so a false-fire rate above about 12\% is excluded at 95\%) and that recall differs by reader. A missed directive costs the user a repetition and a false lesson costs more, which is the intended trade. These utterances are written by the implementer, so the table measures conformance to the specification, not accuracy on users.

\paragraph{Speech understanding without a transcript (N1, failed).}
A projector trained to put the recogniser's encoder features into the language model's embedding space produced fluent persona answers that were often about the wrong topic: on \SpeechInTurns{} held-out spoken questions the word F$_1$ against the text path's answer was \SpeechInF{} from speech against \CascadeF{} for the cascade, and the answer named the topic in \SpeechInTopic{} against \CascadeTopic{} (run \code{speech-in-20261009}).
The gate was not met and no redesign has been trained, so the cascade is the speech path of this paper.
