\section{Discussion, limits and ethics}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the results do not show.}
Teaching is measured on utterances we wrote, fed as text in the main study; the spoken path is a demonstration of three conversations in one synthetic voice. Nothing is known yet about how
people phrase a correction, how often they correct, whether they say yes to a restatement, or what a recogniser does to the names and numbers that matter most in a correction (in the spoken demonstration it heard ``atlas seven'' as ``Atlas SIV'' and ``quartz'' as ``court's'', and
the agent kept what it heard, which is why a restatement is spoken back). Procedures and rules rest on a handful of asks; two rules taught together were not both obeyed; a procedure is stored and told, but nothing yet checks that the agent \emph{does} its steps with tools.
Selection of what to tell the model is overlap of content words, which fails on paraphrase that shares no word with the teaching; the ledger is a prompt addendum, so a teaching also costs context at every turn it is selected, and a very large ledger will need a better selection. A taught
fact is trusted over what the model believes; a user who is wrong makes the agent wrong. Within a conversation a retraction is not enough by itself, because the model's own earlier replies still follow what was taken back (seen in a development run); the agent therefore also tells the model what was taken back.
The speech lessons (\cref{sec:formalism}) are applied for pronunciation only; imitation and supervised lessons are kept but not used, there is no trained naturalness judge, and the optimiser has been shown to learn a planted reward, not naturalness.

\paragraph{Threats to validity.}
All speech so far is synthetic and all recognition is of synthetic speech. A single recogniser is a poor pronunciation judge. A prompted persona is not a trained one,
and one voice seed is not a population of voices. Latency was measured once. A reward that a learned judge defines can be hacked; the probes of \cref{sec:evaluation}
exist for that, and the human preference test on the frozen exam is the guard that cannot be.

\paragraph{Ethics and consent.}
A synthetic voice is always declared as a portrayal, in the type that describes a speaker (a speaker cannot be constructed without one), in every report and in a
spoken statement at the start of a session. A user's recordings are kept local and named by digest; a lesson never holds audio. A user's voice is not cloned without
their instruction, and a lesson can be removed together with its effect.
