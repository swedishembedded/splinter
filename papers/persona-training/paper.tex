\begin{abstract}
We describe and evaluate an autonomous pipeline that takes one natural-language
sentence (``Learn to think like \ldots\ based on the materials \ldots\ in directory
\ldots'') and a directory of an author's writings, and produces a LoRA-adapted
language model. The policy is Qwen3-8B in non-thinking mode, a separate Qwen3-14B
is the judge, and all work runs on two 24~GB Tesla P40 GPUs. The pipeline plans,
captures sources, splits them by \emph{family} (all printings of a text), generates and
verifies tasks, teaches, builds a dataset that mixes verified dialogues with the author's
own text, fine-tunes, examines the result on held-out families, and applies a release gate.
Thomas Jefferson and Samuel Adams serve as test subjects.

The paper is mainly about method: how such a system is measured, and the defects that
were found in it. We report a split rule that survives overlapping editions, an exam
design whose primary comparison is the base model under the same persona prompt and whose
size is chosen by simulation, controls on a model judge, and defects found by audit: a
release gate whose ``held-out'' improvement was mostly paraphrases of trained tasks, a
retention check whose bound lay inside its own noise and whose grader failed correct long
answers, a monitoring split that silently dropped 27\% of the author's voice records, and
training-loop mechanics (per-record loss normalisation, a learning rate that never
annealed under early stopping, sampling with replacement).

The results of the earlier exams are pilot scale. On six exams
over three task sets (31--42 tasks, 6--7 families each) no candidate is distinguishable from
the base model given the persona in its prompt: the family-level sign test against that arm
has $p\geq0.109$ in every exam, and with at most seven families it could reach $\alpha=0.05$
only through a clean sweep of at least five discordant families. The candidates were judged
right on 0 to 8 more tasks than the prompted base. On the two Jefferson task sets the persona
prompt alone cut the number of answers stating specifics absent from the source from 20 to 3
and from 21 to 6.

Two pilots on the first 25 families (155 tasks) of a 39-family, 244-task exam reserved
before any generation then compared each candidate under the persona prompt with the
prompted base, the comparison fixed with the exam, under a calibrated greedy judge. The
v7 candidate was judged right on 38 tasks against 31 (+4.5 points, 95\% interval
$-1.3$ to $+10.0$; family-level sign test $p=0.21$), which is not significant. The
ablation candidate, trained with voice data, was judged right on 48 against 31 (+11.0
points, interval $+5.0$ to $+16.9$; 13 of 15 discordant families, $p=0.004$); the
difference persists among length-matched tasks ($p=0.032$). Neither candidate asked
without the persona prompt exceeds the prompted base (27 and 30 against 31 of 155), so
the pilots do not show that the persona was internalised rather than amplified by the
prompt. From their discordance and intraclass correlation the simulated power of the
full 39-family exam for a ten-point gain is 0.97 and 0.94.

Two further candidates were run on the same pilot subset with only the deployed arm
and paired by task with the prompted arm of the v7 pilot. One, trained on the v7 data
under the corrected recipe, was judged right on 44 tasks against 31 (+8.4 points,
interval $+2.9$ to $+13.5$; 12 of 15 discordant families, $p=0.018$; task-level
$p=0.005$); the other, trained with template-prompted voice rows, on 35 against 28 of
147 (+4.8 points, interval $-1.8$ to $+11.6$; $p=0.30$ over families). Of the four
candidates run on the reserved exam, two (the ablation and this corrected-recipe candidate,
both with fixed-prompt voice rows) are positive at pilot scale, and two (v7, the same voice
rows under the earlier recipe, and the template-voice-rows candidate) are not.

The two positive candidates were then run on the whole frozen exam with all four arms
(242 of its 244 tasks over 38 of 39 families; the rest are the tasks of one family, two tasks,
that the candidates were trained on), without voice scoring. Under the persona prompt the
ablation was judged right on 80 tasks against 58 for the prompted base (+9.1 points, interval
$+3.7$ to $+14.3$; 19 of 24 discordant families, $p=0.0033$) and the corrected-recipe
candidate on 75 (+7.0 points, interval $+1.2$ to $+12.6$; 18 of 25 families, $p=0.022$). The
first remains significant after a Bonferroni factor of four for the candidates examined on the
reserved exam ($p=0.013$); the second does not ($p=0.087$), and its length-matched subset is not
significant ($p=0.068$). Neither candidate asked without the persona prompt exceeds the base
(50 and 53 against 48 of 242) or the prompted base (against 58), so the persona is not shown to
be internalised. The full exam contains the pilot (64\% of its tasks), the candidates were
chosen after the pilot and after earlier exams, and on the 87 tasks outside the pilot the leads
(+5.7 and +4.6 points) are not significant ($p=0.19$ and $0.27$): the full runs repeat the
direction of the pilots at smaller size and do not independently confirm them. One seed was
trained per candidate, there are no human labels, and voice scoring was not run.
\end{abstract}
