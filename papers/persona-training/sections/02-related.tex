\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Models that take on a character or a population.}
Character-LLM trains language models to embody specific historical characters from
constructed profiles and experiences and evaluates how well they retain character knowledge
\citep{shao2023characterllm}. RoleLLM contributes a framework (profile construction,
instruction generation, style imitation, fine-tuning) and a dataset for benchmarking and
improving role-playing \citep{wang2023rolellm}, and \citet{shanahan2023roleplay} propose
role-play as a framing for the behaviour of dialogue agents. For groups of people,
\citet{santurkar2023whose} quantify how far language-model opinions align with 60 US
demographic groups using opinion polls, \citet{argyle2023outofone} show that a model
conditioned on demographics can emulate the response patterns of human subgroups, and
Twin-2K-500 releases answers of 2,058 participants to over 500 questions for building and
validating digital twins \citep{toubia2025twin2k500}. The reference in these evaluations is
retained knowledge of a character or survey answers. Here the reference is the output of a
program whose beliefs are hidden from the model and fixed by construction.

\paragraph{Inferring principles and preferences from behaviour.}
Apprenticeship learning recovers an expert's unknown reward from demonstrations
\citep{abbeel2004apprenticeship}, maximum-entropy inverse reinforcement learning gives a
distribution over decisions \citep{ziebart2008maxent}, and deep reinforcement learning from
human preferences learns tasks from comparisons of trajectories \citep{christiano2017deep}.
Constitutional AI trains assistants from a list of principles
\citep{bai2022constitutional}, and Inverse Constitutional AI extracts interpretable
principles from preference data \citep{findeis2025inverse}. Our principles arm puts a
list of principles in the prompt; the benchmark asks whether the principles can instead be
left implicit in the author's decisions and still be taken up by a fine-tuned model. \citet{treutlein2024connecting} show that language models can infer and
verbalise latent structure from scattered implicit information in their training documents
without in-context learning. We test decisions rather than verbalisation, in the same
spirit: the beliefs of our authors are never stated in the corpus and are implicit in what
the narrator does.

\paragraph{Fine-tuning and retrieval for new knowledge.}
\citet{ovadia2023finetuning} compare unsupervised fine-tuning with retrieval-augmented
generation \citep{lewis2020rag} for injecting knowledge and find retrieval consistently
better for both existing and new knowledge. \citet{allenzhu2023physics31} show in a
controlled study that knowledge must be augmented, for example paraphrased, to be reliably
extractable in pretraining, and \citet{yang2024syntheticcpt} propose synthetic continued
pretraining to learn from small specialised corpora. Our retrieval arm is the corresponding
baseline for judgment: the base model is handed the essay paragraphs that share the most with
the situation under BM25 \citep{robertson2009bm25}. The adapter here is trained on supervised
decision records read out of the essays, not on the essays themselves.

\paragraph{Compositional hold-out.}
SCAN shows that sequence-to-sequence models generalise shallowly and fail systematic
composition \citep{lake2018scan}, and COGS evaluates generalisation to novel combinations in
semantic parsing \citep{kim2020cogs}. We apply the same discipline to judgment: about a fifth of
the combinations of facts and options, and two of twelve topics, are never told in any essay.

\paragraph{Tool use.}
Toolformer trains language models to decide when and how to call external APIs
\citep{schick2023toolformer}, Gorilla fine-tunes a model to write API calls
\citep{patil2023gorilla}, and ToolLLM provides an instruction-tuning dataset from more than
16,000 real APIs \citep{qin2023toolllm}. The Berkeley Function Calling Leaderboard grades
calls by comparing their abstract syntax trees \citep{patil2025bfcl}. We use the call as the carrier
of a decision and grade the option it names; the tool schema is a held-out axis, so that a
decision taken correctly through a withheld schema cannot have been copied from the form of a
training call.

\paragraph{Synthetic training data and evaluation statistics.}
Self-Instruct bootstraps instruction-following data from a model's own generations
\citep{wang2023selfinstruct}, and phi-1 is a small code model trained on textbook-quality and
synthetic data \citep{gunasekar2023textbooks}. Our corpora are model-written, but the
decisions in them are fixed by an oracle and checked by code. LoRA \citep{hu2022lora}
supplies the adapter. For statistics we report a Wilson interval \citep{wilson1927probable}
with every accuracy and Newcombe's hybrid score interval for differences between cells
\citep{newcombe1998interval}, as error bars are advised for language-model evaluations
\citep{miller2024errorbars}, and treat the choice of significance test as a decision to be
justified \citep{dror2018hitchhiker}: the paired sign test is exact, assumes no distribution,
and uses only whether each of two arms was right on an item.
