\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

Every number in this section is made from the files in \code{data/} by
\code{scripts/build\_data.py} (\cref{sec:reproducibility}). Each examination has \ExamItems\ items;
chance is one third.

\subsection{Accuracy of the five arms}\label{sec:accuracy}

\Cref{tab:main} and \cref{fig:accuracy} give the number of items right for each arm and
author. The adapter has the highest count for every author: \RightATuned, \RightBTuned\ and
\RightCTuned\ of \ExamItems\ items (\AccATuned, \AccBTuned, \AccCTuned), against
\RightABase, \RightBBase\ and \RightCBase\ for the base model with the same prompt. The
base model's accuracy is close to the chance level of one third for authors 202 (\AccBBase) and
303 (\AccCBase); for both, the Wilson interval contains one third. Giving the base the author's name changes little: \RightANamed, \RightBNamed\
and \RightCNamed\ items. Giving it the author's four beliefs in order, as statements, raises the
count for author 101 (\RightAPrinciples\ items) and 202 (\RightBPrinciples), and not for author
303 (\RightCPrinciples, against the base's \RightCBase). Retrieval of six essay paragraphs lifts
the base by \LiftARetrieval, \LiftBRetrieval\ and \LiftCRetrieval\ items to \RightARetrieval, \RightBRetrieval\
and \RightCRetrieval, against the adapter's lifts of \LiftATuned, \LiftBTuned\ and \LiftCTuned.

\subsection{Paired comparisons}\label{sec:paired}

\Cref{tab:paired} gives, for the six comparisons, the number of items on which only the first
arm was right, the number on which only the second was, and the one-sided exact sign test.
\begin{itemize}
\item The adapter exceeds the base, the named base and the base with principles for every
  author, with $p<10^{-10}$ in each of the nine tests; for author 303 against the base,
  for example, it is right alone on \OnlyFirstTunedBaseC\ items and wrong alone on
  \OnlySecondTunedBaseC.
\item Against retrieval the adapter's lead is significant for author 202 (\OnlyFirstTunedRetrievalB\ against
  \OnlySecondTunedRetrievalB, $p=\PTunedRetrievalB$) and author 303 (\OnlyFirstTunedRetrievalC\ against
  \OnlySecondTunedRetrievalC, $p=\PTunedRetrievalC$). \emph{For author 101 it is not}: \OnlyFirstTunedRetrievalA\ items
  against \OnlySecondTunedRetrievalA\ among \DiscTunedRetrievalA\ discordant items, $p=\PTunedRetrievalA$.
  The adapter's lead over retrieval is \LeadPtsTunedRetrievalA, \LeadPtsTunedRetrievalB\ and
  \LeadPtsTunedRetrievalC\ points for authors 101, 202 and 303. Adding the discordant items of the
  three examinations gives \PooledFirstTunedRetrieval\ against \PooledSecondTunedRetrieval\
  ($p=\PooledPTunedRetrieval$); this pooled test treats the 480 items of three examinations as one
  sample and says nothing about authors other than these three.
\item Retrieval exceeds the base for every author ($p<10^{-6}$).
\item The principles arm exceeds the base for author 101 ($p=\PPrinciplesBaseA$) and, more weakly, 202 ($p=\PPrinciplesBaseB$);
  for author 303 it does not (\OnlyFirstPrinciplesBaseC\ against \OnlySecondPrinciplesBaseC,
  $p=\PPrinciplesBaseC$).
\end{itemize}
The comparisons are not corrected for multiplicity. With a Bonferroni factor of \PairedTests\
(all paired tests of the table), the largest $p$ among the \ClaimedTests\ comparisons
listed above as significant for the adapter and for retrieval (the adapter over the base, named
and principles arms for every author, the adapter over retrieval for authors 202 and 303,
retrieval over the base for every author) is \BonferroniClaimedMax, below 0.05; the principles
arm over the base becomes $p=\BonferroniPrinciplesA$ for author 101 and $p=\BonferroniPrinciplesB$
for author 202.

\subsection{The adapter's errors}\label{sec:errors}

Which decisions does the adapter get wrong? \Cref{tab:errors} counts, for its wrong answers,
the option the oracle called for and the option the call took. For authors 101 and 202 the
commonest error is to accept the outside offer where the oracle acts alone
(\OfferForAloneA\ of \WrongATuned\ and \OfferForAloneB\ of \WrongBTuned\ wrong answers); for
author 303 it is to act alone where the oracle takes the matter to the assembly
(\AloneForAssemblyC\ of \WrongCTuned). Authors 101 and 202 both hold the lax reading of
\code{self\_reliance}, which favours accepting help, at the lowest priority, so acceptance is
their decision only where the three beliefs above it leave the offer open. We conjecture that
the adapter has taken up the preference for accepting without all of the conditions that
override it; no experiment here tests this.

\subsection{Divergence between authors}\label{sec:divergence}

Where two authors decide a situation differently, a model that has taken up its own author's
judgment should take its author's decision and not the other's. For each adapter,
\cref{tab:cross} counts, over the items of its own author's examination on which a compared
author is determined and decides otherwise, which decision the adapter's call took. The
compared authors are the other two trained authors and the author holding every one of the
trained author's beliefs the other way, in the same order. The counts come from the tuned
arm's answers of \cref{sec:accuracy}; no further training or prompting was involved. In all
nine rows the adapter takes its own author's decision more often than the compared author's,
and more often than neither. Against the opposite author, the compared author's decision is
taken on 0 to 11 of 120 to 156 items. Against the other trained authors it is taken on 1 to 17
of 49 to 97 items, most often by Kalian Fenley's adapter, which takes Maren Selmere's decision
on 17 of 49.

\subsection{Extraction audit}\label{sec:audit}

The extraction of \cref{sec:mining} does not consult the oracle, so its decisions can be
compared with the oracle afterwards. Each kept decision is matched to its episode in the corpus
plan by its article and case (a case name can recur in different articles of the same topic),
and \cref{tab:audit} counts the kept decisions equal to the oracle's. \AuditDisagreeA,
\AuditDisagreeB\ and \AuditDisagreeC\ of them disagree (\AuditDisagreeAll\ of \AuditKeptAll,
\AuditDisagreeRateAll\%); the adapters were trained on all kept decisions, these included.
Every one of these essays had already passed the reading gate of \cref{sec:corpus}, in which
the same model's reading of every decision agreed with the oracle, so a disagreement here is a
second reading of the same essay that differs from the first. Of the decisions with a
fixed-wording briefing (\cref{sec:records}), 3, 3 and 0 have at least one fact reported
differently from the episode's.

\subsection{Belief wording in the essays}\label{sec:echo}

The belief gate of \cref{sec:corpus} turns away nine consecutive words of a belief text as it
is written; it does not catch the wording of a private note put in the narrator's voice.
Counted by \code{scripts/echo.py}, \EchoA, \EchoB\ and \EchoC\ of the \CorpusAEssays,
\CorpusBEssays\ and \CorpusCEssays\ kept essays of authors 101, 202 and 303 repeat six
consecutive words of one of their author's own notes once ``you'', ``your'' and ``yourself''
are put in the first person, for example ``I act at once to protect them, and I account for it
afterwards'' (\cref{lst:retrieval-full}). Likewise, the brief gate compares the phrase for
what the narrator did as it is given, in the second person, and kept essays contain its
first-person form, ``I settled it myself, at once, without waiting''
(\cref{lst:retrieval-full}). The essays do not state the beliefs as rules or maxims, but some
come close to stating the dispositions they were written from; the adapters were trained on
decisions read from these essays, and the retrieval arm was given their paragraphs, as they
are.

\subsection{Cost}\label{sec:cost}

The corpus tool estimates tokens at four characters per token from the text it sends and
receives; by that estimate a corpus run, writing and gate readings together, took
\CostInputA, \CostInputB\ and \CostInputC\ million input and \CostOutputA, \CostOutputB\ and
\CostOutputC\ million output tokens for authors 101, 202 and 303. The hosted provider's
reported daily spend of the account rose by \SpendA\ US dollars during the run for author 101
and by \SpendBC\ US dollars during the joint run for authors 202 and 303; the extraction runs
of \cref{sec:mining} are not included. Training took \TrainHoursA, \TrainHoursB\ and
\TrainHoursC\ hours to step 240 (\cref{tab:training}).
