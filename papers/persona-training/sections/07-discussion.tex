\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the adapter does.}
For each of three authors, an adapter trained on decisions read out of the author's essays
takes the author's decision on \AccATuned, \AccBTuned\ and \AccCTuned\ of examination items,
against one third for a uniform guess and \AccABase, \AccBBase\ and \AccCBase\ for the same model
without the adapter. Every item is a case absent from the training data, and 140 of the 160
items also have a withheld topic, combination of facts and options, or tool schema.
The lowest of the adapter's six per-axis cells is above the highest cell of the base, named
and principles arms for every author (\cref{sec:cells}). Only one of nine seen-minus-new gaps
differs from zero by its interval: author 202 on the withheld tools. What the measurements
establish is this behaviour on the examination: the adapter takes the oracle's decision on
withheld topics, combinations and tools far more often than the unadapted model. They
establish nothing about the form in which the adapter holds the beliefs; no experiment here
reads the beliefs out of the weights.

\paragraph{Does this support the thesis?}
By the rule of \cref{sec:stats}, yes for each of the three authors: the adapter exceeds the
named and the principles arm after the Bonferroni factor, and its lowest cell is above every
cell of those arms. The thesis is supported, not proved; it rests on three authors, one
training run each and one policy model.

\paragraph{The strongest alternative explanation.}
In this world an author's judgment is a function of six boolean facts and of which three of
four options are offered. An adapter that had learned a table from fact patterns to options,
without anything one would call judgment, would score the same. Two observations bound this
explanation without removing it. The combination axis withholds a fifth of the fact and option
combinations from every essay, and the adapter's accuracy on them does not differ from its
accuracy on the others by more than the intervals of \cref{tab:gaps}, so a table copied from
the training records does not account for the results. And the training records restate the
facts in the examination's own sentences when the reader reported them all
(\cref{sec:records}), which the prompted arms never see: part of the adapter's lead may come
from this match of wording, and no experiment here removes those records to measure how much.
What the results establish is that the adapter reproduces the oracle's decisions on
combinations, topics and tools absent from its training data, not how it represents them.

\paragraph{Information in the prompt.}
The base model told the author's name moves little (by \LiftANamed, \LiftBNamed\ and
\LiftCNamed\ items). The base model given the author's four beliefs as statements in order of
priority reaches \AccAPrinciples, \AccBPrinciples\ and \AccCPrinciples. The adapter exceeds it
for each author by at least \LeadPtsTunedPrinciplesA\ points (author 101; more for the
others) without the beliefs in its prompt. The principles prompt states the beliefs in prose
but not when each belief favours or forbids which option, nor how the oracle combines them;
the measurements show that the base model given this prompt does not reproduce the oracle's
decisions, and they do not separate the causes (the prose of the statements, the missing
verdict rules, or how the model applies an order of priority).

\paragraph{Retrieval.}
The retrieval arm gives the base model, at answer time, the six essay paragraphs that share
the most vocabulary with the situation under BM25. Its count rises by \LiftARetrieval,
\LiftBRetrieval\ and \LiftCRetrieval\ items over the base model, more than the named or the
principles prompt gives. The adapter's lead over retrieval is \LeadPtsTunedRetrievalA,
\LeadPtsTunedRetrievalB\ and \LeadPtsTunedRetrievalC\ points; for author 101 the discordant items
split \OnlyFirstTunedRetrievalA\ to \OnlySecondTunedRetrievalA\ ($p=\PTunedRetrievalA$), so for
that author the measurements show neither that fine-tuning beats retrieval nor that retrieval
beats fine-tuning. Retrieval's accuracy varies more across the three authors (\AccARetrieval,
\AccBRetrieval\ and \AccCRetrieval) than the adapter's (\AccATuned, \AccBTuned\ and \AccCTuned).
The study contains no ablation of the retrieval arm, so it does not say which content of the
retrieved paragraphs the model uses, nor why retrieval is stronger for one author than for
another.

\paragraph{Divergence.}
Adapters trained on different authors take their own author's decision more often than the
compared author's where the two disagree, in every one of the nine comparisons, including
against the author holding every belief the opposite way (\cref{tab:cross}). The largest
share of the compared author's decisions is Kalian Fenley's adapter taking Maren Selmere's
decision on 17 of 49 items; these two authors hold two beliefs in the same reading (the
strict reading of \code{haste}, first in both orders, and the lax reading of
\code{self\_reliance}). Which decision an adapter takes where authors disagree thus depends,
in these comparisons, on the author whose essays it was trained on.

\paragraph{Errors.}
The adapter's errors are not spread evenly over the options (\cref{tab:errors}). For the two
authors whose lowest-priority belief favours accepting outside help, most errors accept an
offer where a higher belief makes the author act alone; for author 303 most errors act alone
where the author would take the matter to the assembly. The examination does not say whether
more data, more steps or a different extraction would remove these errors.

\paragraph{Extraction noise.}
\AuditDisagreeAll\ of the \AuditKeptAll\ decisions the reader extracted
(\AuditDisagreeRateMin\% to \AuditDisagreeRateMax\% per author) disagree with the oracle, and
the adapters were trained on them. The decision in a training record is the one the reader
extracted, and every essay it was extracted from had been kept only after an earlier reading
by the same model agreed with the oracle on every case. No adapter
was trained on audited decisions, so the effect of the remaining noise on accuracy is not
measured.

\paragraph{Scope of validity.}
The essays are written by a language model, not by a person, from case files that fix the
facts and the decision, and they are kept only when a reading of them agrees with the oracle,
so every kept essay tells the decision the oracle dictates for each case it narrates; a
person's writing is not filtered this way. The writer, the gate's reader and the extracting
reader are one model, so an error that model makes consistently in writing and reading would
not be caught by the gates. The beliefs are four of six formal tenets applied by a fixed rule,
and the world is the same for every author: twelve topics, six tenets, four ways to act and
fixed sentences for the facts. A withheld topic is a different subject within that world, and
a withheld tool is a different schema for the same kind of call; the study does not test a
different world or a different kind of judgment. The examination grades the option chosen,
not the reason given, and says nothing about the author's style or voice. The quotation gate catches recitation of a belief, not a paraphrase of one, and some kept
essays echo the wording of their author's private notes in the first person
(\cref{sec:echo}). Each author was trained once, with
one policy model of 8B parameters, so the intervals describe the examination's sampling of
items and not the variation between training runs, and three authors are three replications,
not a sample from which a rate across authors can be estimated. The retrieval arm is a single
configuration, BM25 over paragraphs with six per situation, and it was added after the first
three arms had been run.
