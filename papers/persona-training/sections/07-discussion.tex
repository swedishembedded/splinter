\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{The exam, not the adapter, was the weakest link.}
The strongest conclusion from this work is about measurement. The statistics the first
system produced were either uninformative (the anchor, whose bound sat inside its noise) or
about something other than the claim (the gate's improvement on paraphrases of trained
tasks; an effect of the prompt credited to the fine-tune). Once those are set aside, the
remaining evidence on the adapters is a set of point estimates at or above the prompted
base that the exams cannot separate from chance. A power calculation shows this without any
run: seven families, one vote each, cannot reject at $\alpha=0.05$ unless every discordant
family goes the same way and there are at least five of them.

\paragraph{What the data support about the adapter.}
Two statements are supported. (i) On the Jefferson task sets a persona prompt alone cuts the
answers with invented specifics sharply (\cref{tab:prompt}), so a fine-tune must be compared
with the prompted base, not the unprompted one. (ii) No candidate is distinguishable from the
prompted base. Two observations are consistent with expectations from the literature but do
not establish them: the candidate trained on dialogues alone equalled the prompted base (v6:
14 against 14), as one would expect of a student trained on a prompted teacher's answers
\citep{askell2021general}; and the candidates that also saw the author's own text were judged
right on 1 to 8 more tasks than the prompted base in all five of their exams, the direction
\citet{chakrabarty2025readers} report for fine-tuning on an author's complete works in a
different setting (a commercial model, human readers). Those five exams use three task sets,
and three of them share one prompted arm, so they are not five independent observations.

\paragraph{Practices.}
Four practices exposed most of the defects and are cheap: reserve the exam before
generating anything; fix the primary comparison before running it; store every per-task
verdict so that analyses can be redone offline and a power analysis can use the exam's own
discordance; and treat each instrument (judge, grader, split, bootstrap) as a thing to be
measured, with controls whose answer is known and a simulation of its false-positive rate.

\section{Threats to Validity}\label{sec:threats}

\paragraph{Construct validity.}
``Judged right'' means that a model judge says the answer gives what a source passage
says in reply to a generated task. That is a proxy for ``thinks like the author''; it rewards
agreement with the passage's content and position, not prose style, and is silent on whether
the reasoning is the author's. The invented-specifics count is a code proxy for fabrication:
it counts numbers and names absent from the source and partly measures length, which was not
recorded for the reported exams.

\paragraph{Judge.}
Agreement with a person has not been measured; the judge was validated on easy controls
only. Five of the six reported exams were judged with sampling at temperature 0.2, so each
verdict is a single draw and the counts carry judge noise of unknown size. Judge and policy
are the same model family, which invites self-preference that we cannot exclude. The same
family also wrote the tasks and the training data, so style and topic distributions of exam
and training tasks are related even though their source families are disjoint.

\paragraph{Pretraining contamination.}
The letters are public domain and probably appear in the pretraining data of the base. The
exam is held out from fine-tuning, not from pretraining. Whether fine-tuning on the author's
text makes memorised letters easier to recall, which would favour the candidate over the
prompted base, has not been measured.

\paragraph{Adaptive reuse of the pilot exams.}
Recipes were developed over several runs examined on the same 31- and 35-task sets. Even if
an effect were observed there, selection on a small shared test set would overstate it. In
the powered exam's protocol the pilot families are a subset of the final test, so a final
result would not be fully unseen.

\paragraph{Statistical validity.}
Seven-unit family-level tests have the power shown in \cref{fig:power}. The family-level
tests of \cref{tab:exams} are twelve uncorrected tests; the one below 0.05 is not
significant after a Holm correction over them. The task-level bounds ignore clustering. The
power simulations assume a discordance and an ICC instead of estimating them, and give every
family the same number of tasks. There is one training seed per recipe, so run-to-run
variance of the adapter is unknown; a second seed of the v7 recipe was trained but not
examined.

\paragraph{Internal validity.}
The recipe changed between candidates (data, learning rate, schedule, loss normalisation,
monitoring), so no pair of rows in \cref{tab:exams} isolates a single change; v6 against
ablation comes closest, and it also changes the number of steps. The judge changed between
the e4 exam and the other exams on its task set.

\paragraph{External validity.}
Two authors, both eighteenth-century American, writing in English, whose corpora are large
(Jefferson) or modest (Adams), one base model, one adapter rank. Nothing here bears on other
languages, modern authors, or authors with little text.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limits}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{No result on the frozen exam.} No arm's run on it has completed; three
  attempted runs of the four-arm exam on it ended in device out-of-memory faults.
\item \textbf{No human labels.} Judge agreement, kappa, false-right and false-wrong rates and
  length bias on real answers are not measured; no labels were collected.
\item \textbf{No judge-free voice measurement.} The held-out text likelihood of each arm and
  the overlap of answers with the training text (8-, 13- and 20-word runs, longest shared
  run) are implemented in the powered exam, which has no completed run. A fine-tuned model
  that quotes its training text is memorising, not thinking like the author; the reported
  exams cannot separate the two.
\item \textbf{One seed per recipe;} no estimate of run-to-run variance.
\item \textbf{No valid serve check} for any Qwen3 candidate: for two the serving binary was
  absent, for the third no served answer was graded.
\item \textbf{Retrieval is not evaluated.} The pipeline has retrieval-conditioned and
  abstention records and an exam arm with retrieved passages, but no reported run used them;
  all results are for a parametric model asked without retrieval. Retrieval alone was measured
  on 70 situations over 13,960 paragraphs: semantic search ranked the source paragraph in the
  top ten for 66\% of them, lexical BM25 for 30\%. The passage library used for
  retrieval-style training is built over all of a run's sources without a held-out filter.
\item \textbf{Which correction mattered.} No training correction (token-normalised loss,
  annealing, weight decay, epochs) has been ablated. They are justified by reading the code
  and by the literature, not by an effect on an exam.
\item \textbf{Throughput.} Without packing, most of the compute on the P40s is padding, which
  bounds the data a run sees.
\item \textbf{``Thinking like'' is not established.} The observable claim would be that on
  text excluded from training the candidate's positions, prose and abstentions are closer to
  the author's than the baselines'. The data here do not establish it.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}

We built and examined a pipeline that learns from one sentence and an author's writings. Its
most useful products are methodological: a family-level split that survives overlapping
editions, an exam reserved before generation and sized by simulation, a judge with controls
and deterministic overrides, and a catalogue of defects with the measurement that exposed
each. With those defects set aside, the evidence is that a persona prompt alone markedly
reduces invented specifics on the Jefferson exams, that no fine-tuned candidate is
statistically distinguishable from the prompted base at the sample sizes measured, and that
the point estimates of the candidates are at or above it. That fine-tuning adds to the
prompt is not established by this work.
