# Draft decision scenarios (for review, not yet frozen)

Each scenario is a documented decision with its reasons. The situation is a
neutral briefing written before the decision; the choice and the reasons are
withheld from the student and kept for scoring. Every quoted reason was
checked as a verbatim substring of the cited line of the source text (36 of 36
for Jefferson, 25 of 25 for Adams). EXCLUDE lists name the passages that must
be removed from training when the scenario is held out. They are first drafts:
some ranges are broad, some editions are listed only where a match was certain.

Legend (Jefferson): Wn = writings-washington-ed-vn.txt; Mn =
memoir-correspondence-miscellanies-vn.txt; Fnnn = works-federal-ed
(OCR text).

# Thomas Jefferson

## S1 id: bank
SITUATION: February 1791. Congress has passed a bill, proposed by the Treasury Secretary, incorporating a Bank of the United States with a multi-year charter and branches. The bill awaits the President's signature. Washington has asked cabinet members for written opinions within days. Jefferson, Secretary of State, is asked whether the President should sign it.
OPTIONS: advise-veto | advise-sign | advise-sign-and-seek-amendment
CHOICE: advise-veto
REASONS:
1. Powers not enumerated are reserved: "I consider the foundation of the Constitution as laid on this ground:" (W7:19906); "of a boundless field of power, no longer susceptible of any definition." (W7:19911)
2. "Necessary" means indispensable, not convenient: "allows only the means which are "_necessary_," not those which are merely" (W7:19993); "all be carried into execution without a bank. A bank therefore is not" (W7:19988)
3. The Convention refused the power: "the whole was rejected, and one of the reasons for rejection urged in" (W7:19981)
4. Veto only when clear: "hang so even as to balance his judgment, a just respect for the wisdom" (W7:20078)
PRINCIPLE: A body of delegated powers may do only what is granted or indispensable to the grant, so mere convenience never justifies enlarging it, yet a balanced doubt is resolved in favor of the other branch. TRANSFER: a relief charity's treasurer wants a profitable consulting arm outside the bylaws because it would aid fundraising; decline unless members amend the bylaws.
EXCLUDE: W7 19875-20090; M4 22870-23070; F045 9540-9830; W9 4008-4022, 6336-6341, 7406-7425 (Anas); M4 19772, 21451, 22166-22175; W5 14822-14834; W6 8362-8380, 8784, 9300-9306; M4 9367-9372.
BASE_GUESSABLE: yes; strict-construction opposition to the bank is textbook. Only the reasons (Convention history, balanced-doubt rule) are non-obvious.

## S2 id: kentucky
SITUATION: Late October 1798. Congress has passed the Alien and Sedition Acts (June-July 1798); prosecutions of Republican editors have begun. Republicans are a minority in Congress and the federal courts are Federalist. Jefferson is Vice President, at Monticello. Madison sits in the Virginia legislature; Breckinridge and Wilson Cary Nicholas will go to the Kentucky legislature, which meets in November. They ask Jefferson to sketch resolutions for Kentucky. Jefferson must decide what the states, and he, should do.
OPTIONS: state-resolutions-asserting-compact | petition-congress-for-repeal | wait-for-elections
CHOICE: state-resolutions-asserting-compact
REASONS:
1. Silence would imply consent: "necessary to be said, in order to avoid the inference of acquiescence;" (W4:10673)
2. Compact theory, no common judge: "has an equal right to judge for itself, as well of infractions as of" (W9:17248); "not been delegated, a nullification of the act is the rightful remedy:" (W9:17396)
3. Keep graded options: "train as that we may not be committed absolutely to push the matter to" (W4:9113); "every measure of error or of wrong, a cause of scission, we are willing" (W4:10688)
4. Secrecy of authorship: "anything, I must decline it, to avoid suspicions (which were pretty strong" (W4:10706)
PRINCIPLE: When a higher authority exceeds its grant and no neutral arbiter exists, a party to the compact must record a clear protest of principle so silence is not read as consent, while holding back the ultimate remedy. TRANSFER: a regional office told by headquarters to adopt an unauthorized reporting rule files a formal written objection reserving its rights, but keeps operating and does not walk out.
EXCLUDE: W9 17225-17500; W4 9108-9116, 9150-9165, 10640-10715; M3 19440-19505; W7 8245-8300; M4 15205-15245.
BASE_GUESSABLE: yes for authorship and stance; no for finer choices (dropping the reservation clause to defer to Madison, W4:10697; secrecy).

## S3 id: louisiana
SITUATION: August 1803. A treaty signed at Paris in April cedes Louisiana to the United States for about fifteen million dollars. Congress meets in October and must ratify and appropriate. Friends write offering differing readings of what the Constitution allows concerning foreign territory.
OPTIONS: ratify-and-seek-amendment | ratify-by-broad-construction | decline-or-renegotiate
CHOICE: ratify-and-seek-amendment
REASONS:
1. No grant of the power: "has made no provision for our holding foreign territory, still less for" (W4:17217); "have done an act beyond the Constitution. The Legislature in casting" (W4:17220)
2. Prefer the safe, precise reading: "that which is safe and precise. I had rather ask an enlargement of power" (W4:17402)
3. Construction erodes the limit: "construction which would make our powers boundless. Our peculiar security" (W4:17404); "blank paper by construction. I say the same as to the opinion of those" (W4:17406)
4. Haste, since the bargain could lapse: "treaty void. A warning amounting to that has been given to them, and" (W4:17385)
PRINCIPLE: When a necessary act lies outside granted authority, take the act but seek ratification and an express expansion of power from the grantor rather than stretching the text, because stretching destroys the limit itself. TRANSFER: a hospital administrator signs an emergency purchase above his spending limit, reports it to the board for ratification, and asks them to raise the limit rather than reinterpret it as "operational".
EXCLUDE: W4 17140-17240, 17270-17420; M4 84-169, 3351, 9798; F086 1685-1716, 1907-1920; W8 1377-1480 (message); same-principle later passages: W6 7045, W8 2023, 3074, 4659.
BASE_GUESSABLE: partly; a model knows he doubted constitutionality and bought anyway, but likely not that he drafted amendment text and then urged silence.

## S4 id: embargo
SITUATION: December 1807. The British frigate Leopard fired on the Chesapeake in June; British Orders in Council of 11 November restrict neutral trade, and French decrees do likewise. American ships and seamen are exposed to seizure and impressment, and many are at sea. Congress sits. The United States has few warships; merchants want trade to continue.
OPTIONS: embargo | war | continue-trade-on-belligerent-terms
CHOICE: embargo
REASONS:
1. The peaceful alternative: "had, by the course of events, become the only peaceable card we had to" (W5:10876)
2. Protect people and ships: "real danger that the great object of the embargo in keeping our ships and" (W5:9467)
3. Trade on their terms is tribute: "three only alternatives; 1, embargo; 2, war; 3, submission and tribute." (W5:13845)
4. Belief in coercion: "her to justice by peaceable means, and the embargo, evaded as it was," (W5:18799)
PRINCIPLE: Faced with war or submission, prefer self-imposed economic withdrawal that keeps your own people and assets out of reach, accepting its cost for a bounded time. TRANSFER: a farm cooperative facing a single abusive grain buyer withholds its crop from sale for a season rather than sue or accept the buyer's price.
EXCLUDE: every embargo-era passage: W5 8172-15470 and 18655-18800; W6 1951-2047, 16278-16295, 18935; W7 13135-13171, 14903-14947; W8 4095, 4414-4579, 5545-5693, 5877-6031, 6416-6459; M4 4985-5660, 6649-6676, 11756-11774, 18471-18517; F094 153-5516.
BASE_GUESSABLE: yes; the embargo is famous.

## S5 id: sedition-discharge
SITUATION: March 1801. Jefferson has just taken office. Under the Sedition Act of 1798, several editors, including James Callender, have been fined and jailed after trials before federal judges who upheld the statute; other prosecutions are pending, including one against William Duane. The Act expires on 3 March 1801. The President controls pardons and the conduct of federal prosecutions.
OPTIONS: discharge-all-sedition-cases | pardon-selectively-on-merit | leave-to-courts-and-expiry
CHOICE: discharge-all-sedition-cases
REASONS:
1. Law void: "because I considered, and now consider, that law to be a nullity, as" (W4:19065)
2. Duty to resist: "worship a golden image; and that it was as much my duty to arrest its" (W4:19067)
3. Co-equal branches: "the law to be unconstitutional, were bound to remit the execution of it;" (W4:19240); "them. Both magistrates are equally independent in the sphere of action" (W4:19236)
4. Uniform treatment: "It was accordingly done in every instance, without" (W4:19070)
PRINCIPLE: An officer who judges a rule void must refuse to carry it out in his own sphere, uniformly and regardless of who the victims are, since each coordinate branch decides constitutionality for itself. TRANSFER: a compliance officer who believes a rule violates the company charter refuses to enforce it against anyone, even though the legal department read it differently.
EXCLUDE: W4 18687-18700, 19025-19090, 19198-19260 (Adams letters); W5 3395-3410, 16215-16232; W6 11760-11790, 16160-16180; W7 4973; M4 1074-1086, 3398-3400; F078 3696-3700, 11234-11266; plus W9 17225-17500 (same view).
BASE_GUESSABLE: yes; the pardons and co-equal-review argument are well known.

## S6 id: no-fast-proclamation
SITUATION: January 1808. Presidents Washington and Adams proclaimed national days of fasting, prayer or thanksgiving; some state governors still do. A clergyman, Mr. Millar, writes asking Jefferson to recommend a day of fasting and prayer for the nation. Jefferson has so far issued none.
OPTIONS: decline-and-explain-privately | issue-nonbinding-recommendation | issue-proclamation
CHOICE: decline-and-explain-privately
REASONS:
1. Federal government barred: "the government of the United States as interdicted by the Constitution" (W5:8694)
2. A recommendation is still authority: "prayer. That is, that I should _indirectly_ assume to the United States an" (W5:8704); "to carry some authority, and to be sanctioned by some penalty on those" (W5:8707)
3. Good for religion: "of religion to invite the civil magistrate to direct its exercises, its" (W5:8712)
4. Rights belong to societies: "and this right can never be safer than in their own hands, where the" (W5:8719)
PRINCIPLE: Public authority may not steer matters outside its grant even by "mere" recommendation, because endorsement from power works as soft sanction; leave them with the bodies that own them. TRANSFER: a national statistics office asked to "recommend" an investment product declines, since its endorsement would sanction savers' choices.
EXCLUDE: W5 8685-8735; W4 14760-14775; W8 2040-2062; M4 4662-4697; W8 4775-4790 (Danbury, wall of separation).
BASE_GUESSABLE: yes; the refusal is famous, though the "recommendation is indirect authority" reasoning is less so.

## S7 id: french-treaty
SITUATION: April 1793. France has executed its king, declared a republic, and is at war with Britain, Holland and Spain. The 1778 treaties of alliance and commerce with France remain in force and include a guarantee of French West Indian possessions. A new French minister, Genet, is about to arrive. Washington asks his cabinet, on 19 April, whether the United States may renounce or suspend the treaties until France's government settles. Hamilton argues they may be voided; Jefferson must give a written opinion.
OPTIONS: treaties-remain-binding | suspend-treaties | renounce-treaties
CHOICE: treaties-remain-binding
REASONS:
1. The nation, not the king, is party: "between the United States and Louis Capet, but between the two nations of" (W7:22034)
2. Moral law binds nations like persons: "and nation, are obligatory on them by the same moral law which obliges" (W7:22051)
3. Only imminent danger releases: "moment of making the contract. If _possibilities_ would void contracts," (W7:22099)
4. Reception of a minister changes nothing: "requires only that we should do nothing; and doing nothing can hardly" (W7:22185)
PRINCIPLE: Obligations bind the enduring party, not its current officers, and only great, imminent danger, not speculative risk, releases a party. TRANSFER: a city that signed a ten-year lease with a firm since acquired and rebranded may not cancel merely because the new owner might later harm it.
EXCLUDE: W7 21985-22340; W9 5560-5720 (Anas: Genet and treaty memorandum), 6624-6780; M4 21200-21240; W3 19320-19330, 19620-19630 (neutrality letters).
BASE_GUESSABLE: probably yes for the outcome (Jefferson favored France, opposed Hamilton); no for the reasoning.

# Samuel Adams

Six sound episodes exist. Every quote was checked by script against the raw files (whitespace collapsed, line ranges as given). Episodes 1, 2, 4 and 6 are institutional texts, so Adams's authorship rests on Wells (secondary) and Hutchinson.

## ADAMS-01 (Boston instructions, May 1764)
SITUATION: May 1764. Boston has elected four representatives to the General Court. Parliament's trade and revenue measures are known from the colonial agent's reports. The Court was not convened to consider them until late in the previous year, so the agent has had little direction. The town appoints a five-man committee, with Adams on it, to prepare instructions. It must decide what the representatives are to press.
OPTIONS: assert_taxation_rights_and_seek_joint_colonial_action | seek_trade_relief_only | leave_to_representatives_discretion
CHOICE: assert_taxation_rights_and_seek_joint_colonial_action
REASONS:
1. "For if our Trade may be taxed why not our Lands ? Why not the Produce of our Lands & every thing we possess or make use of ?" (cushing-1:783-785)
2. "If Taxes are laid upon us in any shape without our having a legal Representation where they are laid, are we not reducd from the Character of free Subjects to the miserable State of tributary Slaves ?" (cushing-1:790-793)
3. "we further desire you to use your Endeavors, that their Weight may be added to that of this Province" (cushing-1:816-817)
PRINCIPLE: Resist a narrowly justified measure at the level of the principle it would establish, and enlist others harmed by the same principle. Analogue: customers told one API field will be changed unilaterally object to the vendor's right to change contracts without consent, and coordinate with other customers instead of seeking a private exception.
EXCLUDE: cushing-1:633-869; wells-1:3465-3600, 3685-3711, 4296-4300, 4700-4710, 5718-5722, 6796-6803
BASE_GUESSABLE: partly. "Protest the Sugar Act" is guessable. A flat denial of Parliament's right to tax, plus a call for colonial union, is not; Wells flags both as firsts (wells-1:3584-3596).
CONFIDENCE: medium. Curated class COMMITTEE_COAUTHORED; the committee had five members. Wells: "This is the earliest public document written by Samuel Adams" (wells-1:3577-3578).

## ADAMS-02 (Massachusetts Resolves, Oct 1765)
SITUATION: October 1765, shortly before the Stamp Act is to take effect. The House has accepted its answer to the Governor's speech. During the recess Adams had drafted a set of statements on the province's rights. On 24 October the House appoints a committee, including Adams, to prepare resolves on its rights and privileges. The House must settle the form of its public statement and what becomes of it afterwards.
OPTIONS: enter_full_rights_resolves_in_house_records | send_humble_petition_only | omit_statement_of_rights
CHOICE: enter_full_rights_resolves_in_house_records
REASONS:
1. Wells, on the town's earlier instructions: "and inalienable rights of the people should be asserted and vindicated, and their assertion and vindication left upon the public records for transmission to posterity" (wells-1:4704-4706)
2. "Ordered, That all the foregoing resolves be kept in the records of this House, that a just sense of liberty and the firm sentiments of loyalty be transmitted to posterity." (cushing-1:1633-1636)
3. "all acts made by any power whatever, other than the General Assembly of this Province, imposing taxes on the inhabitants, are infringements of our inherent and unalienable rights" (cushing-1:1603-1607)
PRINCIPLE: A body whose rights are newly challenged should state them in a durable, public, authoritative record before the dispute hardens. Analogue: a standards committee whose scope is threatened by a proprietary extension writes its charter into the ratified specification, not a private complaint.
EXCLUDE: cushing-1:1508-1636; wells-1:4560-4585, 4700-4850, 5280-5290, 5530-5538, 5780-5792, 6440-6447
BASE_GUESSABLE: partly. Rights resolves are guessable; entering them as a record for posterity is only half-guessable.
CONFIDENCE: medium. The curated copy (cushing-1-4) is EDITOR_ATTRIBUTED. Wells says Adams prepared the resolves and sat on the committee (wells-1:4700-4723), and "Hutchinson distinctly ascribes this paper, as well as the celebrated resolves subsequently offered in the House, to Samuel Adams" (wells-1:4568-4570).

## ADAMS-03 (standing committee of correspondence, May 1773)
SITUATION: May 1773, the opening of a new House. Adams has been chosen Clerk. The Speaker lays before the House letters from the Virginia and Rhode Island assemblies, enclosing Virginia's March resolves proposing that each assembly appoint a committee to correspond with its counterparts. Adams had earlier thought of proposing intercolonial committees but held back until others were ready.
OPTIONS: appoint_standing_committee_of_correspondence | send_thanks_and_defer | refer_to_special_committee_for_one_reply
CHOICE: appoint_standing_committee_of_correspondence
REASONS:
1. "The Colonies are all embarkd in the same bottom. The Liberties of all are alike invaded by the same haughty Power" (cushing-3:2055-2057)
2. "We have heard of Bloodshed & even civil War in our Sister Colony North Carolina ; And how strange is it, that the best Intelligence we have had of that tragical Scene, has been brought to us from England !" (cushing-3:2070-2074)
3. "Whether the Establishment of Committees of Correspondence among the several Towns in every Colony, would not tend to promote that General Union, upon which the Security of the whole depends." (cushing-3:2097-2101)
PRINCIPLE: When the opposing side acts in concert and one's own side learns of events piecemeal, build a standing channel for sharing information. Analogue: maintainers of interdependent projects, repeatedly surprised by upstream changes, set up a permanent cross-project liaison group.
EXCLUDE: cushing-3:2036-2135; wells-2:1000-1015, 1061-1065, 1126-1140, 3315-3430, 3515-3582, 3676-3682, 3745-3835
BASE_GUESSABLE: yes for the broad fact (famous). The 15-member standing form is less guessable.
CONFIDENCE: high. Wells: "on motion of Samuel Adams, the following resolves were accepted" (wells-2:3751-3752). The reasons come from Adams's own letter to R. H. Lee (curated DRAFT_IN_HAND), dated 10 April, before the decision.

## ADAMS-04 (refusal to rescind, June 1768)
SITUATION: June 1768. The Governor sends the House Lord Hillsborough's letter, which requires the House, in the King's name, to rescind the resolution behind the February circular letter to the other colonies and to declare disapproval of it. If the House refuses, the Governor must dissolve it. The House's request for a recess to consult constituents has been denied. More than a hundred members sit, and the circular letter has already been sent and answered. A committee including Adams considers the demand.
OPTIONS: rescind_and_disavow | refuse_to_rescind_and_justify | adjourn_without_a_vote
CHOICE: refuse_to_rescind_and_justify
REASONS:
1. "We could as well rescind those letters as the resolves ; and both would be equally fruitless" (cushing-1:9624-9625)
2. "we take it to be the native, inherent, and indefeasible right of the subject, jointly or severally, to petition the King for the redress of grievances" (cushing-1:9640-9643)
3. "If the votes of the House are to be controlled by the direction of a minister, we have left us but a vain semblance of liberty." (cushing-1:9710-9713)
4. Candidus (1771): "Indeed the essential rights of all were involved in the question : A different determination would therefore have been to the last degree infamous and attended with fatal consequences." (cushing-2:8923-8926)
PRINCIPLE: Do not concede under threat a point whose concession would surrender a standing right; put the reasoning on the public record. Analogue: a board ordered by a regulator, on pain of sanctions, to retract a report declines and publishes its method, because retraction would concede the power to dictate findings.
EXCLUDE: cushing-1:9108-9762, 14406-14414, 14834-14836, 15008-15012; cushing-2:1813-1815, 1955-1957, 8790-8945, 9013-9040, 14462-14464; cushing-3:3247-3253; wells-1:9850-10152, 11368-11373, 16706-16708, 16799-16801, 20956-20958, 21809-21811; wells-2:2624-2636
BASE_GUESSABLE: yes. It is a famous episode ("the glorious ninety-two").
CONFIDENCE: medium. The curated copy (cushing-1-41) is EDITOR_ATTRIBUTED, but Adams sat on the committee (cushing-1:9511-9514). Wells: "Again employing the pen of Samuel Adams" (wells-1:10060). Candidus (cushing-2-39) is PSEUDONYMOUS_ATTRIBUTED; Wells credits Adams (wells-1:10121).

## ADAMS-05 (Boston tea resolves, 5 Nov 1773)
SITUATION: 5 November 1773. Boston expects the East India Company's tea, still subject to Parliament's duty. Two days earlier, a gathering at Liberty Tree failed to get the appointed consignees to resign. Philadelphia has already adopted eight resolves against the duty and asked its own agents to resign. Adams is a selectman, and a draft preamble for the town's resolutions survives in his hand. The town meeting must decide how to frame its stand.
OPTIONS: adopt_philadelphia_resolves_and_send_committee_to_consignees | draft_new_original_resolves | petition_governor_for_relief
CHOICE: adopt_philadelphia_resolves_and_send_committee_to_consignees
REASONS:
1. "Sense of the Town cannot be better expressed on this Occasion" (cushing-3:3738-3741)
2. "the disposal of their own property is the Inherent Right of Freemen ; that there can be no property in that which another can of right take from us without our consent" (cushing-3:3743-3746)
3. "a direct tendency to render Assemblies useless, and to introduce Arbitrary Government and Slavery" (cushing-3:3757-3759)
PRINCIPLE: When a respected peer has already stated one's position well, adopt it by explicit reference instead of drafting a competing text; this signals unity and saves time. Analogue: a standards body facing a security flaw adopts another body's published advisory with a short local preamble.
EXCLUDE: cushing-3:3715-3801; wells-2:5134-5240
BASE_GUESSABLE: no for the adopt-by-reference mechanism; yes that Boston opposed the tea.
CONFIDENCE: medium. The curated copy (cushing-3-25) is DRAFT_IN_HAND; the note says the preamble draft is in Adams's handwriting (cushing-3:3718-3720). The eight resolves are Philadelphia's, so his position is the decision to adopt them. Wells (wells-2:5222-5226) records the adoption but does not name the drafter.

## ADAMS-06 (Massachusetts resolve against separate accommodation, July 1782)
SITUATION: Early July 1782, with treaty talks under way in Paris. Sir Guy Carleton and Admiral Digby have tried to induce Congress to accept a separate treaty, and Congress refused to negotiate except with its ally. Adams, President of the Massachusetts Senate, has intelligence of private agents sent into America to influence opinion. The legislature is about to adjourn. The question is whether Massachusetts should speak for itself.
OPTIONS: pass_unanimous_state_resolve_rejecting_separate_accommodation | leave_matter_to_congress | authorize_exploratory_talks_with_britain
CHOICE: pass_unanimous_state_resolve_rejecting_separate_accommodation
REASONS:
1. "detaching some of these United States, or some bodies of men therein, from the common cause, and from a connection with our illustrious ally" (wells-3:7831-7833)
2. "we engaged in the present war with a solemn determination to secure, if possible, the rich blessings of freedom to the present and future generations" (wells-3:7852-7854)
3. Wells's gloss: "to justify prompt action in so prominent a State as Massachusetts, whose example thrown early into the scale would do much to counteract such designs" (wells-3:7812-7815)
PRINCIPLE: When an adversary probes for a gap between allies, close it early and publicly with a unanimous statement from a prominent member. Analogue: a consortium member told that a regulator is courting members for side-deals announces it will not negotiate separately.
EXCLUDE: wells-3:7783-7873, 7875-7890
BASE_GUESSABLE: largely yes for the stance. The mechanism (a state-level resolve before adjournment) is less so.
CONFIDENCE: medium. Wells-only (not in the curated set). Wells: "he drew up a resolution expressing the sense of the Massachusetts Legislature" (wells-3:7819-7822); the text is signed "SAM. ADAMS, President" (wells-3:7865). Reason 3 is Wells's inference, not Adams's.
