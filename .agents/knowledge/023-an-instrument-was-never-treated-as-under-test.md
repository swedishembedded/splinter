# 023. An instrument was never treated as under test

Four faults of the measuring apparatus rode along unseen. A judge calibrated
only on controls that were the reference itself or an off-topic text scored
perfect precision, which said nothing about an on-topic answer that adds or
contradicts a specific. The judge decoded with a temperature and no seed, so one
answer could be judged two ways. The first exams stored only arm marginals and
family-level tests, so the per-task analysis could not be redone. And a
family-level test over seven families, one of them a single task, weighed a task
of one family as much as ten of another and could not reach significance.

How to avoid it: calibrate a judge on hard controls (on-topic answers that are
wrong), decode it greedily, store every answer and verdict of every arm so that
an analysis can be redone without a model, and size the test by simulating its
own false-positive rate and power on the units it will really have. Controls
with a known answer, rescoring of saved answers and a simulation of the test
are all measurements of the instrument.
